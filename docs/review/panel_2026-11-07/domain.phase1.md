## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by methodology): The study design, the handling of data, the reporting of any statistics, and the material offered for reproduction must reach the standard a peer reviewer in this field expects. From a domain-accuracy standpoint I read this as the place where experimental and proof-checking procedure is judged; I do not score it, but a domain claim that rests on an inadequately reported procedure is something I will note for the methodology seat rather than absorb into my own score.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by domain): Every claim must agree with what the field currently knows; prior work must be described faithfully, with credit and scope stated correctly; and domain terminology and known results must be used without factual error. This is my dimension: I judge whether the positioning against existing theory and empirics on memorisation, empirical-optimal velocity fields, and guidance is correct and complete, and whether the stated novelty survives comparison with the literature.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's advocate, also eligible for methodology): The central thesis must hang together, the evidence offered must actually support the stated claims, and no reasoning fallacy may undercut the main argument. From my perspective, coherence failures often surface as a gap between what a cited prior result says and what the argument needs it to say; I will leave the scoring to the eligible seats.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owned by perspective): Framing, definitions, and implications must be readable by people in neighbouring fields, and any claim reaching across disciplines must be backed. I do not score this, but I recognise that correct domain terminology is a precondition for accessible framing.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by the editor-in-chief): The organisation, clarity of exposition, quality of figures and tables, and conformity to venue conventions are assessed here. This is outside my scoring remit; domain-level misstatements I find belong under D2, not D5.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief): The work must suit the configured venue and offer an original, significant contribution for its readers. I do not score this dimension; my D2 judgement of what is genuinely new versus already known is an input the editor-in-chief may draw on, but the fit and significance verdict is theirs.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether results presented as new are distinguished from results already established in the flow-matching, diffusion memorisation, closed-form denoiser, and guidance literatures; whether each cited prior work is characterised accurately in assumptions, scope, and conclusions; whether domain terms (empirical optimal velocity field, memorisation, classifier-free guidance, conditional versus marginal flows) are used in their standard meaning; and whether claims about how real trained samplers behave are kept separate from claims about idealised or exact fields.
what_triggers_block: A central novelty claim is shown to be already established in prior work that the manuscript omits or misattributes, or a key prior result is misrepresented in a way that the main conclusion depends on, but the core contribution could survive after repositioning and correction.
what_triggers_warn: Prior work is cited but its scope or assumptions are stated imprecisely, relevant closely related work is missing without affecting the central claim, or domain terminology is used loosely or in a non-standard way that could mislead readers without invalidating any result.
what_triggers_fatal: The principal claimed contribution is substantively identical to an existing published result, or rests on a factual error about established domain theory, such that no repositioning or correction leaves an original and accurate core claim.
