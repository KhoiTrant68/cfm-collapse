## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by methodology). For a proof-based theory submission, rigour means that every stated theorem, proposition and lemma has hypotheses that are stated explicitly and actually used, that each proof step follows from what precedes it, and that limit passages (in time, in a parameter, or in a sample size) are justified by a named mechanism such as uniform bounds, dominated or monotone convergence, compactness, or an explicit rate. Because statistical sampling standards do not apply to pure theory, the reproducibility affordance shifts to two things: whether a competent reader can re-derive each argument from the text and appendices, and whether the numerical checks are specified well enough (integrator, step size, tolerances, stopping rule, seeds, positive and negative controls, code or data availability) to be rerun and to discriminate between the claim and its negation.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by domain). The paper's statements about the surrounding literature, standard definitions and known results must be correct and fairly represented; terminology must match established usage in the relevant mathematical and machine-learning subfields. From the methodology seat this matters where a proof imports an external result: the imported statement must match the cited source's hypotheses, though scoring this dimension belongs to the domain reviewer.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by devil's advocate, methodology eligible). The central thesis must be internally consistent across abstract, theorem statements, proofs, numerics and conclusions; the logical dependency graph among results must be acyclic; quantifiers and scopes (for all versus there exists, generic versus every, finite time versus limit) must be preserved when results are summarised; and evidence offered for a claim must be of the right logical type (a proof for a theorem, a numerical observation only for a conjecture or illustration). Over-inference, circularity, and silent strengthening of claims between statement and summary are the main failure modes.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owned by perspective). Definitions, framing and implications should be readable by adjacent communities (for example dynamical-systems analysts reading machine-learning theory, or practitioners reading analysis), and any claim that a result transfers across disciplines must be substantiated rather than asserted.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by eic). Organisation, exposition, figure and table quality and adherence to venue conventions; from a methodology perspective this includes whether proofs are locatable, whether notation is consistent, and whether the boundary between proved, conjectured and numerically observed statements is visible on the page.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by eic). Whether the work fits the configured venue and makes an original, significant contribution for its readership. This seat makes no venue-fit judgement; it only notes that a contribution's significance depends on its proved scope being what the paper claims it to be.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: Complete hypothesis lists for every result and evidence each is used; correctness of energy or Lyapunov estimates, Gronwall-type bounds and Liouville or divergence-based volume arguments; explicit regularity assumptions (Lipschitz, integrability, bounded-variation of time-dependent coefficients) at the singular endpoint; justified interchange of limits, especially passage to the terminal time t -> 1 and uniformity in initial data; correct treatment of threshold and nonhyperbolic parameter values separately from the hyperbolic case; fast-slow or invariant-manifold (Lyapunov-Perron, Fenichel) reductions applied only where their spectral-gap and smoothness hypotheses hold; numerics with stated integrator, step-size control near the singular endpoint, explicit stopping rule, positive controls showing the diagnostic can detect the predicted effect, negative controls, and a clear separation of finite-time observations from asymptotic claims; any result the authors flag as unchecked or conjectural kept out of the proved chain.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing proof step contains an unjustified limit interchange, an estimate that is not uniform where uniformity is needed (for example near t -> 1 or near a nonhyperbolic threshold), an invariant-manifold or singular-perturbation theorem invoked outside its hypotheses, or a numerical experiment used as primary evidence lacks a stopping rule or positive control so that it cannot discriminate the claim from its negation, and the gap is plausibly repairable within a revision.
what_triggers_warn: Proofs are correct in substance but omit routine justifications (measurability, well-posedness up to but not including the endpoint, constants' dependence), threshold or boundary cases are handled only by remark, a result the authors themselves mark as unchecked is cited downstream with a caveat, or numerical checks are adequately designed but under-reported (missing tolerances, seeds, sensitivity to step size or terminal-time cut-off).
what_triggers_fatal: A headline theorem is false or its proof has an unrepairable gap, demonstrated by a counterexample consistent with the stated hypotheses, by a step that requires a hypothesis contradicting another stated hypothesis, or by a central limit claim at t -> 1 that only the finite-time analysis supports and that cannot be recovered without changing the theorem's conclusion.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Consistency of quantifiers and scope between theorem statements, abstract, introduction and conclusions; acyclic dependency among lemmas and theorems; clear labelling of each claim as proved, conditionally proved, numerically observed or conjectured; numerical evidence used only to illustrate or to probe conjectures, never as a substitute for a missing proof step; consistent definitions of the key objects throughout; claimed classifications (for example of limiting profiles or of intervention outcomes) shown to be exhaustive and mutually exclusive when presented as such; counterexamples or borderline cases acknowledged rather than ignored.
what_triggers_block: The summary claims systematically exceed what the theorems prove (for example a generic or almost-everywhere result reported as holding for every case, or a finite-time result reported as a limit result), a claimed exhaustive classification omits a case that the paper's own hypotheses allow, or a conclusion depends on a statement the paper elsewhere labels unchecked, and fixing it requires restating headline claims.
what_triggers_warn: Isolated wording that overstates scope, inconsistent use of a defined term across sections, a dependency between results that is implicit rather than stated, or numerical observations phrased with more certainty than their status supports, each fixable by local rewording without changing any theorem.
what_triggers_fatal: The central thesis is internally contradictory or circular, for instance a key result's proof assumes its own conclusion directly or through a chain of lemmas, or two headline claims cannot both hold under the paper's own stated hypotheses.
