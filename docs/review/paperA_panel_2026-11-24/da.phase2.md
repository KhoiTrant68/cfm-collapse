## Review Body

**Scope of this card.** I reviewed the TMLR build of the manuscript (the non-ICLR branches), including all appendices; LaTeX comments were not reviewed. No criteria binding was supplied for this run, so I make no venue-alignment claim. I found no text in the manuscript addressed to reviewers or attempting to steer the review. The block on D3 is repairable: it rests on a headline and practical framing that strictly exceeds the formal results, and it can be cured by narrowing the claims and correcting one remark, without any new mathematics.

**Core thesis as I read it.** The paper claims that, within the exact empirical conditional flow-matching field, the number of combined exact fields and the signs of their coefficients decide whether the endpoints leave the training set: label noise, positive mixtures (label averaging), guidance with fixed strength or bounded schedules of bounded variation, and every fixed combination of two fields keep the endpoints on training atoms almost surely; signed combinations of three fields, in particular the conjunction of compositional generation, can put positive probability on circumcentres of training examples, above the explicit threshold 1/sinh^2 B at a two-atom tie, with a spectral criterion in any dimension and a sign rule (a subtracted intermediate field is necessary) at two-atom ties. The practical reading is that, for an audit, composition is the operation that can blend training records, while the other knobs only reweight them.

**Strongest counter-argument.** The organising claim, that the number and signs of combined exact fields decide whether endpoints leave the training set, is better described as a property of strictly positive priors than of field counting. Under the paper's own hard-conditioning setup (h = 0, identifying labels, Proposition 4.2 on exact conditional collapse), each conditional field is exactly the straight-line field to one atom, so any fixed positive average of two such fields drives every source, deterministically and in every geometry, to the weighted average of the two atoms. Label averaging over two training labels, and an unguided conjunction of two training conditions, therefore blend at h = 0 with two fields and no subtraction at all. With label noise the same averages keep the atoms only because a strictly positive prior lets each posterior still collapse onto one atom near the data end; the subtracted intermediate field is the h > 0 mechanism by which that collapse can be undone at a tie. On this reading the three-field blend is the residue of a starker h = 0 phenomenon, smoothed by label noise to an exponentially small threshold, rather than a new boundary set by counting. Second, the practical significance rests on the exact optimum being informative about trained samplers, and the paper's own learned experiments point the other way everywhere beyond a two-dimensional second-moment check: the CIFAR-10 U-Net does not track the label-noise reference (across-condition slope 0.36 against 1) and leaves 17 to 28 percent of its samples off the training images at the cutoff; standard CFM networks never reach the blend and at 5 and 10 w_c leave 0.70 and 0.79 of their sources far from every atom and from the centre; reproducing the blend needed an input centred at a tie chosen with knowledge of the answer. A sceptic concludes that the theorems are correct statements about an object that trained conditional samplers do not approximate in the regimes that matter for auditing.

**Logic-chain validation, theorems to claims.** I traced the headline statements back to their formal counterparts. The positive results are stated with their hypotheses: the theorem on positive field mixtures needs a probability measure on strictly positive priors, the guidance-schedule theorem and the two-field corollary need h > 0 (or priors in the positive simplex), and the negative-guidance corollary needs h > 0. The abstract mostly carries the qualifier ("strictly positive priors"), but the thesis sentence of the abstract ("The answer depends on how many exact fields are combined, and with which signs"), the corresponding sentences in the introduction ("Combinations of two fields only reweight the training examples. Three fields can blend them."), the first sentence of the conclusion, and the last sentence of the abstract (the audit separation) are unqualified. The paper's own Table 1 contains a two-field counterexample to the unqualified version (averaging identifying hard-label fields at h = 0), and the remark in the subsection "What leaves the support" then minimises it by asserting that this happens only in configurations such as the symmetric tie. That assertion is incorrect: by Proposition 4.2(a), exact conditional collapse, the hard-label field at y^i is (x^i - x)/(1 - t) with no posterior weighting, so a position-independent average of hard-label fields with weights lambda and 1 - lambda has the explicit trajectory (1 - t) x_0 + t z with z the lambda-weighted average of the atoms, for every source and every configuration, and z is a circumcentre only for equal weights on two atoms. The same reasoning shows the exact conjunction at h = 0 blends for every guidance strength w at or above zero near a mutual-nearest pair, since there the averaged conditional slope at the tie is zero. This is finding M1. It does not touch the correctness of any h > 0 theorem; it changes the headline rule and the practical recommendation (iii) that composition is a boundary marker with an exponentially small threshold, because in the hard-label regime, which is how most conditional models are trained, blending by averaging is generic rather than marginal. For class labels shared by many images (non-identifying labels at h = 0) the conditional and unconditional priors have different supports, a case the paper states is not covered, so the practical statements in "What this means in practice (i)" and in the broader impact statement that negative guidance and bounded guidance schedules only reweight the training examples are not supported there either; they should carry the h > 0 or identifying-label qualifier.

**Checks that held.** I verified the identification of the equal-weight conjunction of Liu et al. with guidance of strength w applied to the average of the K conditional fields; the closed form of F_w and the threshold 1/sinh^2 B; the edge-midpoint threshold N/(2 tanh^2(B/2)) - 1, which for the triangle at B = 2 gives 1.586 against w_c = 1.159, a ratio 1.368 as printed; the simplex face threshold ratio 1.407 at an edge; the box thresholds 0.076 and 3.68; and the algebra of the two-atom proposition (parts a and b), where the quadratic vanishing at the extreme posterior means bounds the signed second moment below one whenever no inner prior is subtracted. The reduction of the two-field corollary to the schedule theorem with the priors exchanged for negative coefficients is also sound. I found no internal contradiction between the h > 0 theorems. The use-of-language-models statement says that the authors' own line-by-line check of the proofs of the two-field corollary, the two-atom proposition and the genericity remark is not yet complete; readers will weigh that sentence, and it should be resolved before submission, but on my reading the two of these three proofs I checked step by step are correct, so I do not treat it as a validity finding.

**Alternative explanations in the learned evidence.** The paper attributes the failure of standard velocity networks to reach the blend to the boundary layer of width (1 - t)^2 at the tie. A competing explanation is generic guidance-amplified learning error: on the triangle the same standard network leaves most sources at no exact candidate at high guidance, so it does not reproduce the exact endpoint law anywhere, not only at the tie. The ablation in which a velocity network with the rescaled input recovers the exact probabilities in most seeds, and the reported mean squared error over the boundary layer (0.67 against 0.0024), do favour the authors' explanation for the tie itself, so I treat this as partly answered. The successful network, however, is told where the tie is; centred elsewhere it fails, and centred at the triangle's centre it leaves the edge midpoints empty while the exact field puts 0.41 of the mass there at 2 w_c and it strays on 0.16 of its sources. The claim that such networks "can match its exact probability" is literally true for the centre occupancy, but the matched statistic is the one marginal of the endpoint law the input was built to resolve. This is disclosed in the text, so I keep it out of the issue tables, but it should not be read as evidence that a learned sampler reproduces the exact composite.

**Possible cherry-picking in the numerics.** The main-text CIFAR-10 composition sentence reports a midpoint fraction "up to 0.29", which is the maximum over the eight largest-margin pairs; the twelve randomly drawn pairs give a mean of 0.042 at 2 w_c, reported only in the appendix. The classifier there also admits the midpoint only above w_c, so the zero fractions below the threshold cannot fail; the paper says so. Neither changes a theorem, but the main text should give the random-pair figure next to the selected-pair maximum. The exact-field stress test now has a positive control that sees an attracting tie, which answers the obvious objection that a scheme biased towards atoms would pass.

**Modelling premise for label averaging.** The abstract states as fact that a network that resolves labels coarsely averages the field over nearby labels, while the label-bottleneck proposition in the appendix shows that the population optimum of a network seeing a coarse label code is a single field with an averaged prior, on which guidance stays atomic. The main text acknowledges both readings, and the composition result is independently motivated by the conjunction, so this is a minor framing correction: the abstract should present field averaging as a sampler-side operation or as one modelling choice, not as what coarse learners do.

**The so-what test.** If the exact field is not what trained image samplers implement, what remains is a clean and, for h > 0, correctly proved negative statement: label noise, positive label averaging, guidance and its schedules, and two-field combinations cannot by themselves move the exact optimum off the training set, so any copying reduction they bring comes from model error. That survives every objection above and is worth publishing. What does not survive in its current form is the unqualified counting rule and the audit separation, which need the positivity qualifier and an honest statement of what happens at h = 0, and the recommendation that composition is the operation an audit should examine, which sits uneasily next to the paper's own conclusion that composition is a boundary marker that trained networks did not reproduce without an oracle input.
