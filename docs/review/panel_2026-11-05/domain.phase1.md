D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat). From a domain-accuracy standpoint, this dimension asks whether the experimental and analytical design would be accepted by people who study memorisation in diffusion and flow-matching models: whether training sets, sample sizes, solver settings, guidance scales and seeds are reported, whether the memorisation or collapse measurements are defined and computed reproducibly, and whether statistical variation is shown. I do not score it, but I will pass domain-specific observations (for example a nonstandard distance or nearest-neighbour protocol) to the methodology seat.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by me). This dimension asks whether what the work says about flow matching, diffusion, empirical-optimal (closed-form) velocity fields and denoisers, classifier-free guidance and memorisation matches the established mathematics and the published empirical record; whether prior results are attributed to the right works and described with their actual assumptions and scope; and whether field terminology (empirical versus population target, conditional versus marginal path, guidance weight conventions, memorisation versus mode collapse) is used correctly.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's-advocate seat). This dimension asks whether the central thesis holds together: whether the stated results actually follow from the stated assumptions, whether the empirical evidence supports the stated claims rather than a weaker or different one, and whether there are logical gaps or overreach. From my side, a coherence problem often appears as a mismatch between a theorem's hypotheses and the regime in which trained models are run, which I will flag to that seat.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owned by the perspective seat). This dimension asks whether readers from adjacent areas (optimal transport, nonparametric statistics, kernel smoothing, privacy and data-extraction research) can follow the definitions and implications, and whether any claimed links to those areas are substantiated rather than asserted.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by the editor-in-chief seat). This dimension asks about organisation, clarity, notation, figure and table quality and adherence to venue formatting conventions. From a domain view, consistent notation for time direction, interpolation schedule and guidance weight matters most, because conventions differ across the flow-matching and diffusion literatures.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief seat). This dimension asks whether the work suits the configured venue and makes an original, significant contribution for its readership. I make no venue-fit judgement here; my input is limited to whether the claimed novelty is real relative to existing closed-form and memorisation analyses of diffusion and flow models.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Correct statement of the empirical-optimal velocity or denoiser for a finite training set (softmax-weighted average of training points under the chosen interpolation path) and of its known behaviour near the data endpoint; correct handling of time and schedule conventions (rectified, variance-preserving, variance-exploding) and of the classifier-free guidance weight convention (w versus 1+w, conditional minus unconditional extrapolation); accurate attribution and scope of prior closed-form, memorisation-versus-generalisation and data-extraction results, including their assumptions (exact empirical minimiser, finite data, deterministic ODE versus SDE sampling, model capacity); correct distinction between memorisation, mode collapse and reduced diversity; image-scale claims consistent with what is known about trained networks departing from the empirical optimum; and coverage of directly competing analyses rather than only distant work.
what_triggers_block: A central domain claim is wrong or materially misstated, for example asserting a convergence, collapse or generalisation property of empirical-optimal flows or guided samplers that contradicts the standard closed-form analysis or established empirical findings, presenting an already-published result about memorisation of the empirical optimum or guidance-induced concentration as new without credit, or extending a result proved for the exact empirical minimiser to trained networks without evidence or caveat.
what_triggers_warn: The core domain content is correct but there are fixable accuracy issues, such as an imprecise or inconsistent time-direction or guidance-weight convention, a prior result cited with slightly wrong scope or assumptions, missing citations to closely related closed-form or memorisation work, loose use of memorisation versus mode-collapse terminology, or image-scale interpretation stated more strongly than the measurement supports.
what_triggers_fatal: The work's main contribution rests on a domain error that cannot be repaired without changing the result, for example a misidentified empirical-optimal field or flow such that the central theorem concerns the wrong object, or a headline result that is a known, already-published fact about empirical flows or guidance presented as the primary novel contribution, with no remaining claim that survives once correct prior work is credited.
