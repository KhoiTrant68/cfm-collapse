## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by methodology). For a theorem-led machine learning theory paper, rigour means every formal statement carries its full hypotheses: the regularity of the vector field, the solution concept used where the field is singular or only measurable in time, existence, uniqueness and maximal-interval claims, and the precise mode of convergence. Every proof must close without hidden steps. Numerical checks must actually test the stated result at the claimed tolerance, and any training experiments must report seeds, sample sizes, architectures, schedules, solvers and variability, so that a competent reader could reproduce the main figures and tables.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by domain). The paper's claims must agree with the current literature on flow matching, diffusion models, memorisation and guidance. Prior results must be stated as their authors stated them, with the same assumptions, and terminology must be used in its accepted sense. From the methodology seat I note only where a misstated prior result feeds a proof step. I do not score this dimension.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's advocate, methodology eligible). The central thesis must follow from the theorems actually proved and the evidence actually shown. Informal claims in the abstract, introduction and conclusion must not exceed the formal statements. Numerical and empirical sections must support the specific mechanism claimed rather than a weaker or different one. Notation and standing assumptions must stay consistent from section to section, so that one result does not silently rely on a hypothesis another section drops.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owned by perspective). Definitions and implications should be readable by adjacent communities, for example dynamical systems, optimal transport, statistics and applied generative modelling. Any claim about what the results mean for those communities must be backed. This is outside my scoring remit.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by the editor-in-chief). Organisation, clear exposition, the quality of figures and tables, and adherence to venue formatting conventions. From the methodology seat I only flag figures or tables that are ambiguous about what was computed. I do not score this dimension.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief). The paper must suit the configured venue and make an original, significant contribution for its readers. No author-confirmed review-target binding is available, so I make no claim about venue fit and do not score this dimension.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: For each theorem, I will check four things. (1) Hypotheses are complete and are the ones used in the proof: regularity of the velocity field in space and time, its behaviour near singular times and near the data points, and boundedness or integrability needed for Gronwall or comparison steps. (2) The solution concept is explicit (classical, Caratheodory, or a limit of flows), and the existence, uniqueness and maximal-interval arguments for that concept are actually carried out. (3) Asymptotic or endpoint claims say which sense of convergence is meant (pointwise, almost every initial condition, in measure, in law) and on which set, and any exceptional set (a stable manifold, Voronoi or decision boundaries, ties) is identified and shown to be null or small in the stated sense. (4) Constants, rates and limits are tracked consistently between statement and proof. For numerical checks I will look for: a stated integrator, step size or tolerance and time horizon, with any treatment of the singular endpoint; a comparison of numerical and predicted quantities with stated error; and evidence that the check could have failed. For training experiments I will look for: dataset sizes, architectures, optimiser and schedules, number of seeds, dispersion measures, how the closed-form or empirical-optimal reference is computed, and released code or enough detail to rebuild it.
what_triggers_block: A headline theorem whose proof has a gap that cannot be repaired locally. Examples: an existence or uniqueness step invoked where the field is not Lipschitz or not integrable near the singular time, with no substitute argument; an interchange of limit and integral or of limit and flow without justification; an almost-everywhere or genericity claim with no measure-zero argument for the exceptional set; or a proof that silently uses a stronger hypothesis than the statement. Also a block: a main empirical claim resting on one seed, or on unreported solver settings, where the reported effect is of the same order as plausible discretisation or seed variability.
what_triggers_warn: Proofs that are correct in substance but locally incomplete or loose. Examples: a missing but routine step, such as an omitted Gronwall constant or an unstated measurability of the flow map; hypotheses stated more strongly than needed, or scattered across the text rather than in the theorem; numerical checks that confirm the result only qualitatively, without error bars, convergence-in-step-size evidence or tolerance reporting; training experiments with few seeds or incomplete hyperparameter reporting that does not overturn the qualitative conclusion; or code or data availability that is promised but not specified.
what_triggers_fatal: A central theorem that is false as stated. Evidence would be an explicit counterexample satisfying all stated hypotheses, or a numerical check reported in the paper that contradicts the theorem it is meant to support. Also fatal: a core proof that is circular, assuming the endpoint behaviour it sets out to prove, so that the main formal contribution has no valid proof and fixing it would require a different result.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether every informal claim in the abstract, contributions list, figure captions and conclusion can be traced to a specific theorem, proposition or experiment, with the same quantifiers and assumptions. Examples: "all", "almost every", "generic", "for small guidance" versus "for any guidance scale", and "empirical" versus "learned" fields. Whether the paper keeps apart what is proved for the exact empirical or closed-form field and what is only observed for trained networks, and whether any bridge between the two (a perturbation, stability or transfer argument) is proved or explicitly labelled as empirical. Whether experiments isolate the claimed mechanism, with ablations or controls that rule out obvious alternative explanations. Whether notation and standing assumptions are stable across sections.
what_triggers_block: The headline thesis is stated more strongly than the theorems support, and the gap matters for the paper's main message. Examples: a result proved only for the exact empirical field is presented as a property of trained models without a proved transfer bound; a claim proved for a restricted parameter range is advertised for all ranges; or experiments are offered as confirming a mechanism when they cannot tell it apart from a named simpler alternative.
what_triggers_warn: Local overreach or inconsistency that does not change the main message. Examples: a caption or sentence that drops a quantifier or hypothesis present in the formal statement; mild notation drift between sections; a missing control or ablation that would sharpen, but is not needed for, the mechanistic claim; or limitations that exist but are understated or hard to find.
what_triggers_fatal: The central thesis contradicts the paper's own formal results or its own reported evidence. For example, a theorem or figure in the paper implies the opposite of the stated conclusion, or two main results cannot both hold under the paper's standing assumptions. The core argument then fails on its own terms.
