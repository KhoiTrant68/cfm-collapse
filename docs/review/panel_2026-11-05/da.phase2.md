## Review Body

Calibration status: `NOT_CALIBRATED`. No target-criteria binding was supplied for this run (criteria_binding_unavailable), so this report makes no venue-alignment claim; the "so what?" points below are adversarial challenges to significance, not a venue-fit judgement.

Genuine strengths, stated once. The exact-field theorems are stated with their hypotheses, the exceptional sets are characterised rather than waved away (the hyperplane example of Proposition B.4), the two-atom threshold w > 1/sinh^2 B is closed-form and checked numerically, and the paper is unusually candid about what it does not show: the abstract itself says the learned-flow observations "do not establish asymptotic endpoint convergence", Theorem 5.1 is labelled conditional, and Appendix C.4 concedes that the certificate agreement is an identity rather than a prediction. I found no internal contradiction between a theorem and the manuscript's own results, no circular definition and no theorem shown false as stated, so nothing here reaches a fatal or block-level break in the logic chain. The attack is on how far the prose carries those theorems.

Strongest counter-argument. A sceptic would say the paper answers a question about an object that no practitioner samples from. The core results are about the exact empirical minimiser, which everyone already knows memorises, and the paper itself concedes that its Section 3 endpoint law "is therefore a reference, not a new identity". What is left is (i) that positive mixtures and nonnegative guidance of that exact field still end on atoms, which is the expected outcome, and (ii) one surprising mechanism, guidance on a label-averaged exact field, whose off-support endpoints are circumcentres of tied training atoms, a finite set of blends fully determined by the training set. That is still memorisation, now of pairs. The paper motivates the composite as an idealisation of guidance on a network that resolves labels coarsely, but its own evidence cuts against the idealisation. The label-averaging pilot finds that learned label averages do not follow the exact ones. A standard velocity-CFM network never reproduces the midpoint basin, at any guidance strength. The only networks that do are given an extra input, asinh(tx/(1-t)^2), that hard-codes the tie location (x = 0) and the exact boundary-layer scale derived from the theorem. On the transfer side, Theorem 5.1 says an endpoint is near its atom unless the scaled error does not vanish; for data prediction this reads "the endpoint is within the late-time prediction error of the atom", which is close to a restatement. Where the learned networks actually disagree with the exact theory (15 to 30 percent off-atom CIFAR-10 samples at h = 4 to 6 against zero for the exact field, image-scale kernel slopes of 0.481 and -0.156 against 1), the paper declines to attribute the discrepancy, yet the abstract reports only the synthetic agreement. On this reading the contribution reduces to a careful catalogue of exact-field ODE behaviour, with one constructed counterexample whose bearing on trained samplers is not shown.

Logic-chain and overgeneralisation notes behind the MAJOR rows. First, the bridge from "a network that resolves labels only coarsely" to the specific operator v_phi (an average of exact fields over shifted labels, with shift u0 comparable to the label gap in the "exponentially small threshold" regime) is asserted, not argued. A network that confuses labels at the scale of the label gap is the regime the threshold remark needs, and nothing in the paper shows trained networks sit there. Second, the successful learned reproduction is not evidence that networks inherit the mechanism. It shows that a network given an oracle coordinate centred on the known tie can fit the exact field. In a real problem the tie locations (circumcentres) are unknown and combinatorially many, so the input cannot be built without the training set geometry. The abstract's "in our runs, trained networks reproduce the off-atom basin only when their input resolves the scale (1 - t)^2 at the tie" is accurate, but read the other way it says ordinary trained networks do not reproduce it. Third, the CIFAR-10 composition check is a constructed configuration rather than a sampler test. Pairs are selected so the midpoint is far from every other image, the bandwidth is reset per pair so that B = 1, and the average runs over exactly the pair's two labels. The paper itself notes that more isolated pairs give larger fractions, so the selection favours the effect. "The threshold holds unchanged on pairs of CIFAR-10 images" is true of that construction and should not be read as holding for CIFAR-10 sampling. Fourth, the intro bullet heading "Learned fields inherit collapse unless their scaled error persists" cannot be falsified: any off-atom endpoint is by definition a persistent scaled error. Read as an empirical claim, it is contradicted at h > 0 on CIFAR-10, where the networks leave the atoms while the exact field does not.

Lower-severity notes (not tabled). The positive-probability effect in more than one dimension, with several tied atoms, is fragile in the paper's own Table 9: in d = 8 the simplex basin is below the 1/2048 resolution at 1.1 and 2 wc and 0.019 at 5 wc. So "any dimension" in Theorem 4.7 should not be read as "practically non-negligible in any dimension". The intro sentence that non-training endpoints of positive probability arise "only" from the listed mechanisms is correctly scoped to the analysed interventions, but the uncovered cases (sign-changing or unbounded schedules, different supports, non-hyperbolic profiles) are exactly the ones practitioners use, which limits the force of the negative results. Finally, the ethics statement's privacy remark should say that the one off-support mechanism found returns blends of two training images. That is arguably still a leak, not a mitigation.

Alternative explanation the paper should rule out: the velocity-CFM failure to reproduce the basin may say nothing about "resolving the scale (1 - t)^2". It may instead reflect that any finite network with a smooth time embedding has no atoms at all (the paper's own Proposition C.2(a)). If so, comparing "midpoint occupancy" at a finite cutoff measures how close a finite-time flow gets to an atom, not whether the network inherits the composite's basin. The data-predicting, rescaled-input network makes the basin visible partly because its output is evaluated through a coordinate that already resolves the singular layer. The paper's ablation separates input from parametrisation but not from this oracle-coordinate explanation.
