# Denoiser / score Jacobians and memorization vs generalization: prior work map and closeness to results (a)-(d)

Search date: 2026-11-11. About 20 search and fetch calls. Most 2026 papers were checked only at the abstract level, and sometimes in a partial full-text read; each entry says which. arXiv IDs beginning 26xx are 2026 preprints.

## Q1. Kadkhodaie, Guth, Simoncelli, Mallat (2024) and follow-ups: what is said about the denoiser Jacobian?

### Takeaway
Kadkhodaie et al. read the denoiser as adaptive shrinkage along the eigenvectors of its Jacobian. For the optimal denoiser, the Jacobian is posterior covariance / sigma^2, and low MSE implies a low-rank Jacobian (a "soft projection"). They do NOT analyse the Jacobian in the memorizing regime, do NOT prove that learned eigenvalues lie in [0,1], and say nothing about eigenvalues above 1 or the Jacobian approaching the identity at small noise. Memorization versus generalization there is measured by train/test PSNR and by whether two networks trained on disjoint subsets agree, not by the spectrum.

### Cited Findings
- Paper: "Generalization in diffusion models arises from geometry-adaptive harmonic representations", Z. Kadkhodaie, F. Guth, E. P. Simoncelli, S. Mallat. arXiv:2310.02557 (v3, Apr 2024). Published at ICLR 2024 as an oral, with an Outstanding Paper award. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02557v3); [ICLR blog awards](https://blog.iclr.cc/?p=740); [Simons Foundation](https://www.simonsfoundation.org/2024/05/07/ccm-and-ccn-researchers-awarded-outstanding-paper-award-at-the-international-conference-on-learning-representations/)
- For simplicity they assume the Jacobian is symmetric and non-negative. This is exact for the optimal denoiser, whose Jacobian is sigma^-2 times the posterior covariance, and "approximately true" for the network. The only [0,1] range in the paper is for oracle shrinkage factors in a fixed basis. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02557v3)
- The network has no bias terms, so f(y) = grad f(y) y. The denoiser therefore shrinks y along eigenvectors e_k(y) by factors lambda_k(y), and both the basis and the factors adapt to y. Small eigenvalues correspond to local invariances. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02557v3)
- Optimal MSE = sigma^2 times the expected sum of the optimal eigenvalues (trace of the Jacobian). Small error therefore implies a low-rank Jacobian, an "effective dimension". In the disk-manifold example, the count of non-negligible eigenvalues grows as noise decreases. The optimal Jacobian is Id + sigma^2 Hess log p, which the paper does not bound. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02557v3)
- Memorization is shown at N = 1 and N = 10, where train PSNR improves with unit slope and test PSNR is poor. At N = 10^5 on CelebA, networks trained on disjoint subsets produce near-identical samples. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02557v3); [ICLR blog](https://blog.iclr.cc/?p=740)
- Follow-up: Zhang, Li, Li, Shi, Wu, Tao, Qu, "Generalization of Diffusion Models Arises with a Balanced Representation Space" (arXiv:2512.20963; ICLR 2026). In a two-layer ReLU denoising autoencoder, memorization gives "localized spiky representations" and generalization gives "balanced representations". This is a statement about representations; the abstract says nothing about the Jacobian. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20963)
- A search-engine snippet attributes to a 2025 paper the claim that, around memorized samples, the Jacobian becomes "noticeably low-rank and dominated by the corresponding data vector", while generalized samples have Jacobians reflecting local data statistics. I could not confirm which paper this is (it may be 2512.20963's full text). — [search snippet pointing to arXiv 2512.20963](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20963)

### Inferences
- The exact empirical denoiser has Jacobian Cov_posterior(x0 | x)/sigma^2. In the CFM convention with xhat = E[x1 | x_t] and x_t = t x1 + (1-t) x0, it is t/(1-t)^2 times Cov_posterior(x1 | x_t). This matrix is PSD, and it tends to 0 once the posterior concentrates on one training point (after "collapse"). The exact empirical field then has d v/dx ~ -1/(1-t) I, which is the user's contraction at rate 1/(1-t). This is a direct consequence of the posterior-covariance identity; the Kadkhodaie line does not state it in this form.
- In the Kadkhodaie framework, a learned eigenvalue above 1 is an expansion of the input and has no "shrinkage" meaning. The user's mu-versus-1 criterion is an axis that the shrinkage literature never uses.

### Gaps
- I did not find a paper that measures sym(J) of a learned denoiser against the threshold 1 along sampling. Nor did I find one showing that learned eigenvalues exceed 1 at small noise.

## Q2. Works that measure Jacobian or Hessian spectra (LID, rank, sharpness, curvature) to detect memorization

### Takeaway
There is a dense 2024-2026 line on this. Every work uses rank, effective rank, spectral gaps or eigenvalue signs of the score Jacobian (= log-density Hessian) as a detection statistic. None compares the symmetric-part eigenvalues of the data prediction with 1, and none gives an endpoint bound. The closest is Jaziri et al. (Nov 2026). They write the x0-Jacobian via Tweedie and the rectified-flow "curvature operator" s dv/dx - I. They study singular-value concentration rather than eigenvalue-versus-1 stability, and they state no theorems.

### Cited Findings
- Jaziri, Mundt, Humayun, Ramesh, "Spectral Signatures of Memorization Basins in Diffusion Trajectories", arXiv:2611.01775 (1 Nov 2026, preprint). They use J_t = d eps_theta/dx_t ~ -b_t H_t and note that dx0_hat/dx_t = alpha_t^-1 (I + b_t^2 H_t). For rectified flow they define J^RF = s dv_theta/dx - I = (1-s) H_s at the optimum. Their statistics are singular values only (spectral gap, stable rank, entropy and Renyi effective rank). They make no comparison with 1 and no contraction claim. A "memorization basin" is defined by reachability under bounded perturbations; the absorption property is given with a proof sketch, and there are no numbered theorems. They report the Jacobian is "nearly symmetric and sign-coherent near the data endpoint". Results: GeoSteer cuts late-stage memorization on CelebA from 51.6% to 7.6% (DDIM) and from 60.2% to 8.0% (rectified flow). JER detection reaches AUROC 0.956 (DDIM) and 0.870 (RF). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01775) (first ~100k characters read); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01775)
- Jeon et al., "Understanding (and Mitigating) Memorization in Generative Models via Sharpness in Probability Landscapes", arXiv:2412.04140 (Dec 2024). They take the Hessian of log p (the Jacobian of the score), use its eigenvalues as a memorization diagnostic, and quantify memorization by counting positive eigenvalues. I did not verify the venue (possibly ICML 2025). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.04140); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386502862_Understanding_Memorization_in_Generative_Models_via_Sharpness_in_Probability_Landscapes)
- Ross et al., "A Geometric Framework for Understanding Memorization in Generative Models", arXiv:2411.00113 (2024; cited by Jaziri et al. as Ross et al. 2025, with FLIPD). They tie memorization to low local intrinsic dimension (LID). They note that singular values are "rarely exactly zero", so counting rank is fragile. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00113)
- Stanczuk et al., "On gauge freedom, conservativity and intrinsic dimensionality estimation in diffusion models", arXiv:2402.03845 (2024). With an exact score, the rank of the Jacobian of the score-like field equals the manifold dimension, which gives an LID estimator. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03845)
- "Localizing Memorized Regions in Diffusion Models via Coordinate-Wise Curvature Differences", arXiv:2605.26756 (2026, preprint). The intrinsic data curvature has to be subtracted using an underfitted or unconditional baseline. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26756)
- "Local Hessian Spectral Filtering for Robust Intrinsic Dimension Estimation", arXiv:2605.01221 (2026, preprint). It reports AUROC 99.87% for LID-based memorization detection on a CIFAR-10 DDPM (search snippet; not opened). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01221)
- Ventura, Achilli, Silvestri, Lucibello, Ambrogioni, "Manifolds, Random Matrices and Spectral Gaps: The geometric phases of generative diffusion", arXiv:2410.05898. Published at ICLR 2025. Gaps in the spectrum of the score Jacobian reveal how many sub-manifolds there are and their dimensions. They describe three phases (trivial, manifold coverage, manifold consolidation) and fit theory against trained-network spectra. — [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/dbdb2729f874e209b56ffecd23aa3b36-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05898v4)
- Achilli, Ventura, et al., "Losing dimensions: Geometric memorization in generative diffusion", arXiv:2410.08727 (Oct 2024; v2 Mar 2026; preprint, venue not verified). Memorization is a progressive loss of dimensions, and high-variance subspaces are lost first. A partial "geometric memorization" can occur without collapse onto a single training point. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.08727)

### Inferences
- The detection literature works with the score Jacobian H. Through Tweedie, sym(dxhat/dx) = alpha^-1 (I + b^2 H). The condition mu < 1 is equivalent to the top eigenvalue of H being below a time-dependent threshold, roughly (alpha - 1)/b^2, or simply H < 0 when alpha = 1. So mu < 1 means the density is locally strictly log-concave along all directions, which is a "sharpness/peak" condition. Jeon et al.'s count of positive or negative Hessian eigenvalues is the nearest empirical relative. Theirs is a heuristic statistic, though, not a threshold that comes with a guarantee.
- Jaziri et al.'s RF operator s dv/dx - I is, in data-prediction form, essentially (s/(1-s)) (dxhat/dx - I), up to how the convention is written. Their "sign-coherent near the endpoint" remark is the empirical counterpart of the user's sign condition on mu - 1. They do not turn it into a bound.

### Gaps
- I did not read the second half of 2611.01775 (appendix A.3 on symmetry). It might contain an eigenvalue-sign analysis.
- Venues for 2412.04140, 2411.00113 and 2410.08727 are not verified.

## Q3. A threshold at eigenvalue 1, or training points as attractors of the learned flow

### Takeaway
The attractor picture is widespread: Biroli et al. 2024, cyclic-denoising papers in 2026, scale-space fixed points, associative-memory views. I found NO work that states the linear-stability criterion "sym(dxhat/dx) < 1 means attracting, > 1 + kappa means at most one trajectory". I also found none that bounds the endpoint distance to a training example by (late error)/(1 - mubar). Results (b) and (c) appear new.

### Cited Findings
- Biroli, Bonnaire, de Bortoli, Mezard, "Dynamical Regimes of Diffusion Models", arXiv:2402.18491 (Feb 2024; published in Nature Communications 2024 per later citations, not verified here). The collapse time t_C is "the time where the backward trajectory falls into the attractor of one given data point". This is a glass transition where P_t fragments into lumps around the training data, computed from the excess entropy under the exact empirical score. Avoiding collapse requires exponentially many data in d. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18491)
- Lopez Amado, Fumero, Locatello, "From Modes to Memories: Characterizing the Scale-Space Dynamics of Diffusion Models", arXiv:2609.39648 (30 Sep 2026, preprint). At fixed sigma they iterate the denoiser as a self-map. For the exact denoiser, fixed points are critical points of the smoothed density and attractors are its modes. The critical scale sigma_c is the largest sigma at which an example is retained. The abstract does not mention Jacobian eigenvalues or contraction. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39648)
- Inference on that paper: in a fixed-point iteration x <- D(x), an attracting fixed point requires a spectral radius of dD/dx below 1. That is a classical eigenvalue-1 criterion, but for a different map (iteration at fixed sigma, not the time-dependent ODE). I did not see it stated in the abstract.
- "Cyclic Denoising Reveals Ultrastable Memories in Diffusion Models", arXiv:2606.24000 (2026, preprint). Memorized examples behave as deep, large-basin states, recovered by repeated noising and denoising. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24000)
- Verma, Dileep, Singh, Sastry, Hebbalaguppe, Ranu, Krishnan, "Early Signatures of Memorization in Diffusion Models via Basin Geometry and Cyclic Denoising", arXiv:2610.11670 (8 Oct 2026, preprint). Basins are measured by score divergence and basin volume. They prove that under the exact empirical score, cycling started near an isolated sample recovers it. Degenerate attractors that match no single training image also appear. On a CelebA checkpoint with 0.1% one-shot copies, 500 cycles raise the memorized fraction above 30%. The abstract mentions no eigenvalue-versus-1 criterion. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11670)
- "Classifier-Free Guidance inside the Attraction Basin May Cause Memorization", arXiv:2411.16738 (2024; reportedly CVPR 2025, not verified). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.16738)
- "Memorization to Generalization: Emergence of Diffusion Models from Associative Memory", arXiv:2505.21777 (2025). With little data there is a distinct attractor per training sample; as data grows the model moves toward generalization. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21777)
- Dodson, Gao, Wang, Wang, Wan, "Two Calm Ends and the Wild Middle: A Geometric Picture of Memorization in Diffusion Models", arXiv:2602.17846 (19 Feb 2026, preprint). Memorization risk peaks at medium noise. Small noise is safe because of limited coverage, and large noise because of low posterior concentration, with a "provably near linear Gaussian" denoiser. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17846)
- A search summary states that the empirical optimal denoiser memorizes almost completely at small noise while a learned EDM model shows little memorization there. The source was not pinned down (possibly 2602.17846). — [arXiv HTML 2602.17846](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17846)
- Targeted searches for "eigenvalue greater than one / repelling fixed point / training sample" returned none of this. — [search, 2026-11-11, sources above]

### Inferences
- Result (c), "smallest eigenvalue > 1 + kappa near a point means at most one trajectory converges there", is a repelling or expansion statement. I found nothing analogous. The basin papers define basins by reachability or volume, not by Jacobian sign.
- The Dodson et al. and Biroli pictures fit the user's (d): late in sampling (small noise), memorization by the exact field is avoided only through network error, and the user's (1-t)^-2 amplification quantifies how that error is magnified.

### Gaps
- Full texts of 2609.39648, 2606.24000 and 2610.11670 were not read. One of them could contain a Jacobian-stability lemma for the exact empirical denoiser. Its closeness to (b)/(c) would need a check before submission.

## Q4. The Jacobian of the exact empirical denoiser (posterior covariance) and collapse or speciation times

### Takeaway
Biroli et al. 2024 define speciation through the top eigenvalue of the data covariance and collapse through excess entropy. They do not define them through the posterior-covariance Jacobian, although the two are mathematically linked. The Ambrogioni group (Ventura et al. ICLR 2025, Achilli et al. 2024) is the closest to a spectral, Jacobian-based phase account. Theirs is about manifold dimensions, not point collapse.

### Cited Findings
- Speciation time is set by the principal-component eigenvalue of the data covariance; collapse time by the entropy of the noised empirical distribution. — [arXiv 2402.18491](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18491)
- Extension to coupled multimodal diffusion: Albrychiewicz, Franco Valiente, Chen, arXiv:2602.04780 (2026, preprint). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04780v1)
- Bonnaire, Urfin, Biroli, Mezard, "Why Diffusion Models Don't Memorize: The Role of Implicit Dynamical Regularization in Training", arXiv:2505.17638. Oral at NeurIPS 2025. tau_gen is constant while tau_mem grows linearly with n, which opens an early-stopping window. — [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/oral/119373); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17638v2)
- Ventura et al.: the phases are read off the spectrum of the score Jacobian. — [ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/dbdb2729f874e209b56ffecd23aa3b36-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Buchanan, Pai, Ma, De Bortoli, "On the Edge of Memorization in Diffusion Models", arXiv:2508.17689 (Aug 2025, preprint). Whether a model memorizes is decided by the training-loss gap between a memorizing and a generalizing model; the crossover is set by model (under)parameterization. The abstract has no Jacobian analysis. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17689)

### Inferences
- Collapse can be read as the time after which tr Cov_post(x1 | x_t) ~ 0, so mu of the exact empirical denoiser tends to 0 and the field contracts. The user's mu-based condition is a pointwise, network-level analogue of Biroli's collapse. That framing seems absent from the literature and is a useful positioning sentence.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the Nature Communications publication of 2402.18491 here.

## Q5. EDM preconditioning (c_skip -> 1) and the learned Jacobian tending to the identity at small noise; Gronwall and one-sided Lipschitz error bounds

### Takeaway
EDM's c_skip(sigma) = sigma_data^2/(sigma^2 + sigma_data^2) tends to 1 as sigma tends to 0. The parameterization therefore biases the learned denoiser towards the identity at small noise. I found no paper that turns this into a statement about the Jacobian or about memorization. In flow-matching convergence theory, Gronwall bounds with Lipschitz or one-sided Lipschitz constants are standard. Result (a) is a known technique applied to a specific data-prediction decomposition, and its novelty lies in the "mu - 1" form, not in the Gronwall argument.

### Cited Findings
- EDM preconditioning formulas (Karras et al. 2022) as restated in later work. One later paper notes that only as t -> 0 (sigma -> infinity) does c_skip -> 0 and the model approach x-prediction; hence c_skip -> 1 at small sigma. — [Back to Basics, arXiv:2511.13720](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.13720); [NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/physicsnemo/26.05/physicsnemo/api/diffusion/preconditioners.html)
- No source analyses the Jacobian or memorization in the c_skip -> 1 regime (search returned none). — [search results above](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.13720)
- Yang et al., "Lipschitz Singularities in Diffusion Models", arXiv:2306.11251. Published at ICLR 2024 as an oral. Networks show near-infinite Lipschitz constants with respect to the timestep near t = 0, and the remedy E-TSDM shares the timestep condition. This concerns d/dt, not d/dx, but it is the closest published "late-time regularity blow-up" result. — [ICLR 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/3aff334b139550ffffed3f4e2498d43b-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11251)
- Albergo and Vanden-Eijnden (2023, Prop. 3) bound W2^2 by the integrated squared velocity error under a uniform spatial Lipschitz bound. Benton et al., "Error Bounds for Flow Matching Methods" (arXiv:2305.16860; TMLR 2024, venue not re-verified), extend this to time-dependent Lipschitz bounds, with a constant depending on their time integral. — [arXiv 2305.16860](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16860); summary in [arXiv 2604.06065](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06065)
- "Distribution estimation via Flow Matching with Lipschitz guarantees", arXiv:2509.02337 (2025). It notes that the Lipschitz assumption can be relaxed to one-sided Lipschitz following Stephanovitch (2025, Prop. 1). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02337)
- Stephanovitch, "Lipschitz regularity in Flow Matching and Diffusion Models: sharp sampling rates and functional inequalities", arXiv:2604.06065 (7 Apr 2026, math.ST preprint). It gives sharp Lipschitz and one-sided Lipschitz regularity of FM fields with optimal time and dimension dependence, a globally Lipschitz transport map, and an Euler error of order sqrt(d)/N. It targets population (smooth) targets, not empirical measures or memorization (abstract only). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06065)
- A search snippet attributed to a 2026 conditional-flow-matching paper says the one-sided Lipschitz constant, defined as the largest eigenvalue of the symmetric Jacobian, would give a tighter bound, but the authors use the full Lipschitz constant L_v instead. I could not identify the paper; candidates were arXiv:2605.13063 and 2605.08398, and 2605.08398's abstract does not mention it. — [search result list](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13063)
- Briq, Kamp, Fried, Cohen, Kesselheim, "Exploring and Exploiting Stability in Latent Flow Matching", arXiv:2605.08398 (ICML 2026). Same noise seed gives similar outputs under reduced data or capacity. No one-sided Lipschitz content appears in the abstract. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08398)

### Inferences: how close is prior work to (a)-(d)?
- **(a) Gronwall with an exp(int (mu-1)/(1-t)) amplifier.** The technique is known: Gronwall bounds with one-sided Lipschitz constants appear in Benton et al., in 2509.02337 and in Stephanovitch 2025/2026. What seems new is the decomposition: the field's own -1/(1-t) contraction is separated from the network's sym(dxhat/dx), so the amplifier is governed by mu - 1 and is less than 1 when mu < 1. The novelty claim should be phrased around this decomposition and its interpretation, not around Gronwall. Moderate overlap.
- **(b) mu <= mubar < 1 late in sampling gives distance to the training example <= (late error)/(1 - mubar), with a sharp constant.** Nothing equivalent was found. The basin and detection papers (2611.01775, 2610.11670, 2412.04140) are empirical or define basins by reachability. Likely novel.
- **(c) lambda_min > 1 + kappa allows at most one converging trajectory.** Nothing found. Likely novel. The nearest related idea is the fixed-point stability of the denoiser map in the scale-space view (2609.39648), which works at fixed sigma and has no expansion result.
- **(d) A (1-t)^-2 ratio of trajectory error to training-law error, with constant E|Z_1-a|^2/(|v(a,1)-a|^2 + d), using the hard-label loss floor d.** Nothing found. Related published facts: the Lipschitz singularities near t = 0 (Yang et al., ICLR 2024; in t, not x), and Dodson et al.'s "calm end" at small noise. Likely novel.
- **Mapping to the Tweedie view.** mu < 1 corresponds to the score Hessian being negative definite in the right scaling. Reviewers will likely say "this is just log-concavity / Hessian sharpness (Jeon et al. 2024)". The paper should cite 2412.04140, 2611.01775 and Ventura et al. ICLR 2025 and explain that those works use spectra as detection statistics without a contraction guarantee.

### Gaps
- Not checked: Gao and Zhu, and other 2024-2025 "convergence of flow-based models" papers that use one-sided Lipschitz along the exact path. These are worth a targeted check for overlap with (a).
- I did not read any of the 2026 basin papers in full. 2611.01775 Appendix A and 2610.11670's exact-empirical-score proof should be read before claiming novelty for (b)/(c).
