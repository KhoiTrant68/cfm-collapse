\begin{abstract}
Conditional flow matching (CFM) trained to convergence on a finite dataset is often
described as ``memorising'' the training data: overtraining is reported to collapse
the conditional generative law onto individual training examples. We give an exact,
population-level account of why this happens. Fixing the dataset and the
deterministic linear interpolant, the $L^2$-optimal velocity field is a
posterior-weighted average of per-example collapsed fields, and under hard
conditioning with identifying labels this average degenerates to a single atom: the
exact minimiser has \emph{zero} conditional variance and zero loss
(Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}). We then show that the standard remedy---label
smoothing, i.e.\ adding Gaussian noise to the conditioning variable---turns this hard
selection into \emph{exact kernel regression over the training atoms}: the generated
endpoint law becomes a Nadaraya--Watson mixture $\sum_i p_i^{(h)}(y)\,\delta_{x^i}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint}) whose moments are a parameter-free reference curve
computable directly from the training set (Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}). This
remedy, however, can only match moments; it cannot recover the posterior, since the
generated law remains atomic at every bandwidth $h$, yielding an $h$-independent
Wasserstein floor (Proposition~\ref{prop:atomicity}). We further show that two
superficially similar remedies are not interchangeable: label noise moves the
population endpoint, while interpolant noise provably does not, at any noise level
(Proposition~\ref{prop:prop17}). Across five seeds, a trained MLP tracks the kernel
reference to within seed noise ($\text{ratio-to-kernel}\approx1.00$ for $h\ge0.05$),
and we reproduce the same collapse on a Gaussian-mixture posterior, on MNIST and
CIFAR-10 inpainting, under adversarially shuffled labels that carry no real
information about $x$, and across a range of architectures and optimisers.
Throughout, we distinguish carefully between exact population consequences and
empirical findings that the population theory does not, by itself, determine.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Flow matching and stochastic-interpolant models are usually trained by regressing a
velocity field against a linear path between a noise sample and a data sample. On a
finite dataset, exact minimisation of this objective against the \emph{empirical}
data law is known to induce memorisation: deployed diffusion models can be made to
reproduce individual training images verbatim
\citep{carlini2023extracting,somepalli2023diffusion}, and a recent line of work has
documented that conditional flow-matching models, trained past the point of good
posterior calibration, produce increasingly deterministic (low-variance) conditional
samples that coincide with individual training examples
\citep{physicscfm2026,gradvar2025}.

We give an exact, population-level account of this mechanism. Fixing the training
set $\D=\{(x^i,y^i)\}_{i=1}^N$ and treating only the interpolation randomness as
random, we characterise the exact population minimiser of the flow-matching
objective over all square-integrable vector fields, together with the endpoint law
of its flow.

The observation that the empirical minimiser memorises is \emph{not} new; it is well
established in the diffusion-memorisation literature
\citep{pidstrigach2022manifolds,gu2023memorization,kadkhodaie2024generalization,biroli2023diffusion,kamb2024creativity,buchanan2025edge}.
Closest in spirit is \citet{buchanan2025edge}, who independently characterise a
memorisation/generalisation \emph{phase transition} in \emph{unconditional}
diffusion models by comparing the training loss of a Gaussian-mixture denoiser at
capacity $M$ to that of a memorising surrogate, as $M$ ranges from the true number
of modes $K$ up to the dataset size $N$, and show that the resulting crossover
point $M_\star$ is linear in $N$. This is a capacity-driven account of the same
broad phenomenon we study from the label/bandwidth side: their $M\colon K\to N$
interpolation is the model-capacity analogue of our $h\colon0\to\infty$
interpolation (Corollaries~\ref{cor:zerobw} and~\ref{cor:infbw}), and neither
result subsumes the other.

Our contribution is the \emph{conditional and kernel-regression structure} underlying
this collapse, which the existing literature does not isolate:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Conditional sharpening.} Hard conditioning turns full-empirical-measure
  memorisation into \emph{single-atom} memorisation---zero conditional variance
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:distinction}). The correct contrast with the unconditional
  model is not ``memorises vs.\ does not'' but \emph{which} empirical measure is
  memorised.
\item \textbf{Label smoothing $\equiv$ kernel regression.} Adding label noise makes
  the population endpoint an exact Nadaraya--Watson mixture over training atoms
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint}), converting a $0/1$ statement into a quantitative,
  parameter-free reference curve $\Covh(y)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}).
\item \textbf{Separation of two remedies.} Label noise changes the population
  endpoint; interpolant noise provably does not, for any noise level
  (Propositions~\ref{prop:prop17} and \ref{prop:factors}).
\item \textbf{Atomicity obstruction.} The smoothed law is atomic for every $h$,
  yielding an $h$-independent Wasserstein floor (Proposition~\ref{prop:atomicity}):
  matching a second moment is not recovering the posterior.
\item \textbf{Representation floor.} A uniform Lipschitz bound on the velocity field
  gives a measurable loss floor $d/(3L)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:floor}) that separates
  representation error from optimisation error.
\item \textbf{Finite-sample vs.\ population representability.} To close a gap
  between the fixed-dataset population theory above and finite-sample training, we
  adapt two representability results of the unconditional gradient-variance/
  stochastic-interpolant literature \citep{gradvar2025} into the present notation
  and setting (Proposition~\ref{prop:memorize}, Remark~\ref{rem:resample}): a fixed
  finite unconditional batch always admits an exact zero-loss memorising field
  despite positive population loss, and training-time resampling of $x_0$ is
  exactly what defeats this construction. Building on this, we give a new,
  problem-specific exact-representability result: no finite-depth, finite-width
  network with globally Lipschitz activations can equal the collapsed field's
  $1/(1-t)$ blow-up \emph{pointwise} on all of $[0,1)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:norepr}).
  We keep this pointwise claim explicitly separate from a strict loss floor:
  unbounded pointwise error on a shrinking neighbourhood of $t=1$ does not by
  itself imply $L_0(v_\theta)$ stays bounded away from $0$, so the only class for
  which we prove a genuine positive loss floor is the Lipschitz-capped one of
  Corollary~\ref{cor:floor}, not an arbitrary finite architecture with unbounded
  weights.
\end{enumerate}

In one line: \emph{conditional collapse is the zero-bandwidth limit of a
kernel-weighted empirical conditional flow.}

A word on scope, developed formally in Section~\ref{sec:scope}. Everything in the
theory characterises the exact population minimiser for the empirical data law; it is
not a statement about what a finite network trained by SGD attains. We therefore
distinguish sharply between quantities the population theory determines (and which our
experiments confirm) and quantities it does not (dependence of observed collapse on
dataset size or observation noise), which we report as empirical findings rather than
``predictions.''

\section{Setup and notation}
\label{sec:setup}

Fix a training set $\D=\{(x^i,y^i)\}_{i=1}^N$ with $x^i\in\R^d$, $y^i\in\R^k$,
empirical joint law $\rhohat_{XY}=\frac1N\sum_i\delta_{(x^i,y^i)}$ and marginal
$\rhohat_X=\frac1N\sum_i\delta_{x^i}$. The training set is fixed (deterministic);
randomness comes only from
\[
I\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\dots,N\},\qquad X_0\sim\pi_0=\N(0,I_d),\qquad t\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1),
\]
drawn independently, together with label noise $\varepsilon\sim\N(0,I_k)$ when
$h>0$. Set $X_1=x^I$ and, for the deterministic linear interpolant,
\[
X_t=(1-t)X_0+tX_1,\qquad U:=\dot X_t=X_1-X_0.
\]
The smoothed label is $\widetilde Y=y^I+h\varepsilon$ with Gaussian kernel
$K_h(u)=(2\pi h^2)^{-k/2}\exp(-|u|^2/2h^2)$. The objective is
\begin{equation}
L_h(v)=\E\big[\,|U-v(X_t,t,\widetilde Y)|^2\,\big].
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
For $h=0$ we write $\widetilde Y=Y=y^I$ and $L_0$. Let $\bar x=\frac1N\sum_i x^i$ and
$\Xhat=\frac1N\sum_i(x^i-\bar x)(x^i-\bar x)^\top$. In the linear-Gaussian instance
used experimentally, $y=Ax+\eta$ with $\eta\sim\N(0,\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}^2 I_k)$,
which yields a Gaussian analytic posterior with covariance $\Sigmapost$.

Scope marker. Everything in Sections~\ref{sec:theory-hard}--\ref{sec:theory-noise}
characterises the exact population minimiser over $L^2$-measurable vector fields for
the empirical data law. Section~\ref{sec:theory-floor} quantifies one reason a finite
trained network differs.
\end{quote}

\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

We state the load-bearing results with proofs; auxiliary lemmas are proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. The projection identity underlying everything is standard.

\begin{proposition}[$L^2$ projection]
\label{prop:projection}
For every square-integrable $v$,
$L_h(v)=\E[\Var(U\mid X_t,t,\widetilde Y)]+\E\,|v-\E[U\mid X_t,t,\widetilde Y]|^2$,
so the a.e.-unique minimiser is $\vstar(x,t,y)=\E[U\mid X_t=x,t,\widetilde Y=y]$ with
$\inf_v L_h=\E[\Var(U\mid X_t,t,\widetilde Y)]$.
\end{proposition}

Two facts make the conditional expectations legitimate and the flows well posed
without any additional assumption (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}): (i) for $t<1$ the map
$X_0\mapsto X_t=(1-t)X_0+tx^i$ is an invertible affine change of variables, with
$X_0=(X_t-tx^i)/(1-t)$ and conditional density
$p(x\mid I=i,t)=(1-t)^{-d}\pi_0\!\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)>0$ for every $x$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cov}); (ii) any field of the form
$v(x,t)=\sum_i w_i(x,t)\,(x^i-x)/(1-t)$ with smooth weights summing to one is
$C^\infty$ hence locally Lipschitz on $\R^d\times[0,1)$, so its ODE has a unique
solution up to $t=1$ and endpoint laws are the well-defined weak limits
$p_1=\lim_{t\uparrow1}p_t$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:wellposed}).

\subsection{Hard conditioning: exact collapse}
\label{sec:theory-hard}

We begin with the base case: a raw, unsmoothed label ($h=0$) that identifies its
training example uniquely. Proposition~\ref{prop:projection} already tells us the
exact minimiser is a conditional expectation; the question is what that conditional
expectation collapses to once the conditioning event pins down a single index $i$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact conditional collapse; $h=0$]
\label{prop:collapse}
Assume $y^1,\dots,y^N$ are pairwise distinct. Then
(a) $v_0^\star(x,t,y^i)=(x^i-x)/(1-t)$ for $t<1$;
(b) $\inf_v L_0(v)=0$;
(c) the ODE $\dot x_t=(x^i-x_t)/(1-t)$ has the explicit solution
$x_t=(1-t)x_0+tx^i$, so $x_t\to x^i$ for every $x_0$; and
(d) $p_1^{\mathrm{cond}}(\cdot\mid y^i)=\delta_{x^i}$, hence
$\Cov[p_1^{\mathrm{cond}}(\cdot\mid y^i)]=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) Distinctness makes $\{Y=y^i\}$ identify $I=i$, so $X_1=x^i$ is deterministic and,
by Lemma~\ref{lem:cov}, $X_0=(x-tx^i)/(1-t)$ is determined by $(x,t,i)$. Hence
$U=x^i-X_0=(x^i-x)/(1-t)$ is $\sigma(X_t,t,Y)$-measurable and equals its conditional
expectation. (b) $U-v_0^\star=0$ a.s. (c) Multiplying by $(1-t)$ gives
$\frac{d}{dt}\big[\frac{x_t-x^i}{1-t}\big]=0$, so $\frac{x_t-x^i}{1-t}\equiv x_0-x^i$.
(d) The endpoint is $x^i$ independently of $x_0$.
\end{proof}

If labels are not distinct, collapse is onto the label's empirical conditional
support: for $\mathcal I_y=\{i:y^i=y\}$, $p_1^{\mathrm{cond}}(\cdot\mid y)=
|\mathcal I_y|^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_y}\delta_{x^i}$. Collapse is always to the
empirical conditional law, which is a single atom exactly when the label is an
identifier.

\begin{proposition}[Unconditional endpoint is the full empirical law]
\label{prop:uncond}
For $L_{\mathrm{unc}}(v)=\E|X_1-X_0-v(X_t,t)|^2$ and $t<1$, with
$q_i(x,t)=\Pr(I=i\mid X_t=x,t)\propto\pi_0\!\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)$,
\[
v_{\mathrm{unc}}^\star(x,t)=\sum_i q_i(x,t)\,\frac{x^i-x}{1-t},\qquad
p_1^{\mathrm{unc}}=\rhohat_X=\tfrac1N\sum_i\delta_{x^i}.
\]
Moreover $\inf_v L_{\mathrm{unc}}>0$ whenever $x^i\ne x^j$ for some $i\ne j$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[The correct conditional/unconditional distinction]
\label{cor:distinction}
Both exact population flows are supported on the training set:
$p_1^{\mathrm{cond}}(\cdot\mid y^i)=\delta_{x^i}$ and
$p_1^{\mathrm{unc}}=\frac1N\sum_i\delta_{x^i}$. The distinction is therefore not
``memorises vs.\ does not'' but \emph{single-example} vs.\
\emph{full-empirical-measure} memorisation, with observable difference in the
conditional second moment:
\begin{equation}
\Cov[p_1^{\mathrm{cond}}(\cdot\mid y^i)]=0
\qquad\text{vs.}\qquad
\Cov[p_1^{\mathrm{unc}}]=\Xhat.
\label{eq:distinction}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The often-cited observation $\tr\Cov\approx\tr\Xhat$ for the unconditional baseline
is precisely \eqref{eq:distinction}, i.e.\ evidence of full-empirical-measure
memorisation, \emph{not} of non-memorisation.

\subsection{Label smoothing is exactly kernel regression}
\label{sec:theory-kernel}

The natural fix for a label that identifies its example too precisely is to blur
it: replace $y^i$ by a noisy $\widetilde Y=y^i+h\varepsilon$ and see what the exact
minimiser does with the resulting ambiguity. We show this is not a heuristic
smoothing trick but an exact statement: the population minimiser becomes the
closed-form velocity field of a Nadaraya--Watson kernel regression over the
training atoms.

\begin{proposition}[Kernel-weighted population minimiser]
\label{prop:kernel-field}
For $h>0$, $t<1$,
\begin{equation}
\vstar(x,t,y)=\sum_i w_i^{(h)}(x,t,y)\,\frac{x^i-x}{1-t},\quad
w_i^{(h)}=\frac{\pi_0\!\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)K_h(y-y^i)}
{\sum_j\pi_0\!\big(\tfrac{x-tx^j}{1-t}\big)K_h(y-y^j)}.
\label{eq:kernel-field}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $(x,t,y)$ with $t<1$. By Bayes' rule applied to the discrete latent index $I$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr(I=i\mid X_t=x,t,\widetilde Y=y)=
\frac{\Pr(I=i)\,p(x,y\mid I=i,t)}{\sum_j\Pr(I=j)\,p(x,y\mid I=j,t)}.
\label{eq:bayes-index}
\end{equation}
Given $I=i$, $X_t=(1-t)X_0+tx^i$ is a deterministic affine function of $X_0$ alone
and $\widetilde Y=y^i+h\varepsilon$ is a deterministic affine function of $\varepsilon$
alone, with $X_0\perp\varepsilon$; hence $X_t\perp\widetilde Y\mid I=i$, with
marginal conditional densities $p(x\mid I=i,t)=(1-t)^{-d}\pi_0\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cov}) and $p(y\mid I=i)=K_h(y-y^i)$ (the density of $y^i+h\varepsilon$).
Independence gives the joint conditional density
$p(x,y\mid I=i,t)=(1-t)^{-d}\pi_0\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)K_h(y-y^i)$; with the
uniform prior $\Pr(I=i)=1/N$, \eqref{eq:bayes-index} becomes
\[
\Pr(I=i\mid X_t=x,t,\widetilde Y=y)=
\frac{\frac1N(1-t)^{-d}\,\pi_0\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)K_h(y-y^i)}
{\sum_j\frac1N(1-t)^{-d}\,\pi_0\big(\tfrac{x-tx^j}{1-t}\big)K_h(y-y^j)}
=\frac{\pi_0\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)K_h(y-y^i)}
{\sum_j\pi_0\big(\tfrac{x-tx^j}{1-t}\big)K_h(y-y^j)}=w_i^{(h)}(x,t,y),
\]
the $i$-independent factor $\frac1N(1-t)^{-d}$ cancelling between the numerator and
every term of the denominator (the kernel normalisation constant inside $K_h$
cancels identically for the same reason). Given $I=i$, one further conditions on
$X_t=x$: by Lemma~\ref{lem:cov} this determines $X_0=(x-tx^i)/(1-t)$, so
$U=X_1-X_0=x^i-X_0=(x^i-x)/(1-t)$, exactly as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}(a). By Proposition~\ref{prop:projection}, the
population minimiser is $\vstar(x,t,y)=\E[U\mid X_t=x,t,\widetilde Y=y]$, obtained
by averaging this per-index target over the index posterior just computed:
\[
\vstar(x,t,y)=\sum_{i=1}^N\Pr(I=i\mid X_t=x,t,\widetilde Y=y)\cdot\frac{x^i-x}{1-t}
=\sum_{i=1}^Nw_i^{(h)}(x,t,y)\,\frac{x^i-x}{1-t},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:kernel-field}.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:kernel-field} factorises the index posterior into a spatial
source-consistency term $\pi_0(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t})$ and a label kernel
$K_h(y-y^i)$. Write $p_i^{(h)}(y):=K_h(y-y^i)/\sum_j K_h(y-y^j)$ for the normalised
label weights, i.e.\ the index posterior obtained from the label factor alone; the
next lemma shows $\vstar$ is exactly the conditional velocity of an explicit,
alternative coupling driven only by these weights, which is what lets us read off
the endpoint law in Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint} below.

\begin{lemma}[Equivalent mixture coupling]
\label{lem:mixture-coupling}
Fix $y$ and consider the coupling $I\sim p^{(h)}(y)$, $X_0\sim\pi_0$ (independent of
$I$), $X_1:=x^I$, with the same linear interpolant $X_t=(1-t)X_0+tX_1$. Its
conditional velocity field $\E[X_1-X_0\mid X_t=x,t]$ equals $\vstar(x,t,y)$ of
\eqref{eq:kernel-field} for every $x,t<1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Under this coupling, $\Pr(I=i\mid X_t=x,t)\propto\Pr(I=i)\,p(x\mid I=i,t)
=p_i^{(h)}(y)\cdot(1-t)^{-d}\pi_0\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)$ by the same Bayes'-rule
computation as above (now with prior $p_i^{(h)}(y)$ in place of $1/N$), and
$p_i^{(h)}(y)\propto K_h(y-y^i)$ by definition, so
$\Pr(I=i\mid X_t=x,t)\propto\pi_0\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)K_h(y-y^i)$---the same
proportionality (in $i$, at fixed $x,t,y$) as the numerator of $w_i^{(h)}$ in
\eqref{eq:kernel-field}; normalising over $i$ recovers $w_i^{(h)}(x,t,y)$ exactly.
As in the proof above, given $I=i$ the target is $(x^i-x)/(1-t)$, so
$\E[X_1-X_0\mid X_t=x,t]=\sum_iw_i^{(h)}(x,t,y)(x^i-x)/(1-t)=\vstar(x,t,y)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint law under label smoothing]
\label{thm:endpoint}
For every $h>0$ and query $y$,
\begin{equation}
p_h^{\mathrm{gen}}(\cdot\mid y)=\sum_{i=1}^N p_i^{(h)}(y)\,\delta_{x^i}.
\label{eq:endpoint}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:mixture-coupling}, $\vstar(\cdot,\cdot,y)$ is exactly the
conditional velocity field of the mixture coupling $I\sim p^{(h)}(y)$,
$X_0\sim\pi_0$, $X_1=x^I$, driven by the same linear interpolant. This field has
the form $v(x,t)=\sum_iw_i(x,t)(x^i-x)/(1-t)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:wellposed}, with
smooth weights $w_i=w_i^{(h)}(x,t,y)$ summing to one (a ratio of smooth, strictly
positive functions of $(x,t)$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:cov}'s positivity of $\pi_0$), so
Lemma~\ref{lem:wellposed} applies: the ODE $\dot x_t=\vstar(x_t,t,y)$ is well posed
on $[0,1)$ for every initial condition, and by the superposition principle its flow
$(\Phi_t)_{t<1}$ transports $\pi_0=\mathrm{Law}(X_0)$ to
$p_t:=\mathrm{Law}(X_t)$ for every $t<1$, where $X_t$ is the coupling's own
interpolant---because $\vstar$ is by construction the coupling's conditional
velocity, $(\Phi_t)_\#\pi_0$ and $\mathrm{Law}(X_t)$ solve the same continuity
equation with the same initial condition, hence coincide by uniqueness. It remains
to identify the limiting law of $X_t$ as $t\uparrow1$: since $X_t=(1-t)X_0+tx^I$
with $X_0\sim\pi_0$ independent of $I$, $X_t\to x^I$ almost surely as $t\uparrow1$
for every realisation of $I$; hence $\mathrm{Law}(X_t)\Rightarrow\mathrm{Law}(x^I)
=\sum_ip_i^{(h)}(y)\delta_{x^i}$ weakly, which is $p_1=\lim_{t\uparrow1}p_t$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:wellposed}. This is \eqref{eq:endpoint}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Zero bandwidth at fixed $N$]
\label{cor:zerobw}
If $y$ has a unique nearest label $i^\star=\arg\min_i|y-y^i|$, then
$p_i^{(h)}(y)\to\mathbf 1\{i=i^\star\}$ as $h\to0$, so
$p_h^{\mathrm{gen}}(\cdot\mid y)\Rightarrow\delta_{x^{i^\star}}$; at $y=y^i$ this
recovers Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $i\ne i^\star$,
$K_h(y-y^i)/K_h(y-y^{i^\star})=\exp\big[-(|y-y^i|^2-|y-y^{i^\star}|^2)/2h^2\big]\to0$
as $h\to0$, since $|y-y^i|>|y-y^{i^\star}|$; normalising the weights $K_h(y-y^j)$
gives $p^{(h)}(y)\to\mathbf 1\{i=i^\star\}$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint} gives the
claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Infinite bandwidth at fixed $N$]
\label{cor:infbw}
$p_i^{(h)}(y)\to1/N$ for every $i$ as $h\to\infty$, so
$p_h^{\mathrm{gen}}(\cdot\mid y)\Rightarrow p_1^{\mathrm{unc}}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$K_h(y-y^i)=(2\pi h^2)^{-k/2}\big[1+O(h^{-2})\big]$ uniformly in $i$ as $h\to\infty$,
so the normalised weights $p_i^{(h)}(y)\to1/N$; apply Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint}.
\end{proof}

Thus $h$ interpolates continuously between the two hard regimes of
Section~\ref{sec:theory-hard}:
\[
\delta_{x^{i^\star}}\ \xrightarrow{\ h\nearrow\ }\ \sum_i p_i^{(h)}(y)\,\delta_{x^i}\ \xrightarrow{\ h\to\infty\ }\ \tfrac1N\sum_i\delta_{x^i}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite-$N$ moments]
\label{prop:moments}
$\bar x_h(y)=\sum_i p_i^{(h)}(y)\,x^i$ and
$\Covh(y)=\sum_i p_i^{(h)}(y)\,(x^i-\bar x_h(y))(x^i-\bar x_h(y))^\top$, both exactly
computable from the training set for any $h$. They are the correct reference against
which generated moments must be compared. $\tr\Covh(y)$ need not be monotone in $h$;
monotonicity is not implied and must be reported empirically.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Label smoothing never leaves the training set]
\label{prop:atomicity}
For every $h\in[0,\infty)$, $\operatorname{supp}(p_h^{\mathrm{gen}}(\cdot\mid y))
\subseteq\{x^1,\dots,x^N\}$. Consequently, if the true posterior $p(\cdot\mid y)$ is
absolutely continuous,
\begin{equation}
W_2^2\big(p_h^{\mathrm{gen}}(\cdot\mid y),\,p(\cdot\mid y)\big)\ \ge\
\int\operatorname{dist}\big(x,\{x^i\}\big)^2\,p(x\mid y)\,dx\ >\ 0,
\label{eq:w2floor}
\end{equation}
\emph{and this lower bound does not depend on $h$.}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The support claim is Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint}. For any coupling $(X,X')$ of
$p_h^{\mathrm{gen}}$ and $p(\cdot\mid y)$, $X\in\{x^i\}$ a.s.\ so
$|X-X'|\ge\operatorname{dist}(X',\{x^i\})$ pointwise; take expectations and infimise.
The right side is the same for every $h$ and is strictly positive because a finite
set is $p(\cdot\mid y)$-null.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:atomicity} is the central caveat on the ``variance is restored
at $h\approx0.1$'' claim: label smoothing \emph{redistributes weight over training
atoms}; it never generates new samples. Matching $\tr\Covh$ matches a second moment,
not the posterior. A genuine remedy requires $h\to0$ \emph{and} $N\to\infty$ jointly,
which is the Nadaraya--Watson consistency regime of Proposition~\ref{prop:nwrate}
below---not $h$ alone.

\begin{proposition}[Bandwidth expansion of the conditional covariance]
\label{prop:expansion}
Let $(x^j,y^j)$ be i.i.d.\ with $\Sigma(y)=\Cov(X\mid Y=y)$ and
$J(y)=\partial\mu/\partial y$. Under standard smoothness and $Nh^k\to\infty,h\to0$,
\begin{equation}
\Covh(y)=\Sigma(y)+h^2 J(y)J(y)^\top+O(h^4)+O_P\big((Nh^k)^{-1/2}\big),
\label{eq:expansion}
\end{equation}
so $\tr\Covh(y)=\tr\Sigma(y)+h^2\|J(y)\|_F^2+\cdots$.
\end{proposition}

The between-group inflation $h^2\|J\|_F^2$ is positive and, unlike the bias of the
conditional \emph{mean}, is not annihilated by kernel symmetry; it produces the
right-hand (over-smoothing) branch of the empirical U-curve. In the linear-Gaussian
model $\mu(y)$ is exactly linear, so $J=\Sigmapost A^\top/\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}^2$ is
known in closed form and the $O(h^4)$ term vanishes: \eqref{eq:expansion} is then a
parameter-free prediction.

\begin{proposition}[Nadaraya--Watson rates for the conditional mean]
\label{prop:nwrate}
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:expansion}, $\bar x_h(y)$ is exactly
the Nadaraya--Watson estimator of $\mu(y)$, with
\[
\mathrm{Bias}=O(h^2),\qquad \Var=O\big((Nh^k)^{-1}\big),\qquad
\mathrm{MSE}=O(h^4)+O\big((Nh^k)^{-1}\big),
\]
minimised at $h^\star\asymp N^{-1/(k+4)}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The bias is the $O(h^2)$ term identified in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:expansion} (Taylor-expanding $\mu(y^j)$ about $y$ against the
symmetric kernel weights); squaring gives $\mathrm{Bias}^2=O(h^4)$. Averaging over an
effective sample size $\asymp Nh^k$ gives the usual Nadaraya--Watson variance rate
$O((Nh^k)^{-1})$. Summing, $\mathrm{MSE}(h)=c_1h^4+c_2(Nh^k)^{-1}+o(\cdot)$ for
constants $c_1,c_2>0$; differentiating and setting to $0$,
$4c_1h^3=c_2kN^{-1}h^{-k-1}$, i.e.\ $h^{k+4}\propto N^{-1}$, so
$h^\star\asymp N^{-1/(k+4)}$.
\end{proof}

Queries at $y=y^i$ are \emph{design points}: $x^i$ receives the maximal weight
$K_h(0)$, and $Nh^k\to\infty$ is exactly what makes its relative influence vanish;
without it Proposition~\ref{prop:nwrate} does not apply there. This is a genuinely
different $h\to0$ limit from Corollary~\ref{cor:zerobw}'s fixed-$N$ nearest-neighbour
collapse: same symbol, opposite conclusions.

\subsection{Stochastic interpolant noise does not help}
\label{sec:theory-noise}

Label noise is not the only place one could inject randomness to try to break the
hard identification of Section~\ref{sec:theory-hard}: an equally natural candidate
is to add noise to the interpolant path itself, as in stochastic-interpolant
constructions. We show this alternative remedy is qualitatively different: it never
touches the population endpoint, at any noise level.

Replace the interpolant by $X_t=(1-t)X_0+tX_1+\gamma(t)Z$ with $Z\sim\N(0,I_d)$
independent and $\gamma(t)=\sigma\sqrt{t(1-t)}$, so $\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)=0$. A single
$Z$ defines the whole trajectory, and the correct pathwise-derivative target is
\begin{equation}
\dot X_t=X_1-X_0+\dot\gamma(t)Z,\qquad \dot\gamma(t)=\frac{\sigma(1-2t)}{2\sqrt{t(1-t)}}.
\label{eq:c2target}
\end{equation}
Regressing on $X_1-X_0$ alone is a \emph{different} objective and does not yield the
marginal velocity of the noisy path. Let $s_t^2:=(1-t)^2+\gamma(t)^2=(1-t)[(1-t)+\sigma^2 t]$,
so $s_0=1,\,s_1=0$.

\begin{proposition}[Closed form and endpoint invariance in $\sigma$]
\label{prop:prop17}
Let $h=0$ with distinct labels and $v_\sigma^\star=\E[\dot X_t\mid X_t,t,Y]$ for
target \eqref{eq:c2target}. Then for $t<1$:
(a) $v_\sigma^\star(x,t,y^i)=x^i+c(t)(x-tx^i)$ with
$c(t)=\frac{-(1-t)+\frac{\sigma^2}2(1-2t)}{s_t^2}=\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\log s_t^2$;
(b) the flow is $x_t=t\,x^i+s_t\,x_0$; and
(c) since $s_1=0$,
\begin{equation}
p_1^{\mathrm{cond}}(\cdot\mid y^i)=\delta_{x^i}\quad\text{for every }\sigma\ge0.
\label{eq:invariance}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Given $I=i$, write $r:=x-tx^i$. Since $X_t=(1-t)X_0+tx^i+\gamma(t)Z$,
$r=(1-t)X_0+\gamma(t)Z$, a linear combination of the independent standard
Gaussians $X_0,Z\sim\N(0,I_d)$, so $r\sim\N(0,s_t^2I_d)$ with
$s_t^2=(1-t)^2+\gamma(t)^2$. For jointly Gaussian $(X_0,r)$,
$\Cov(X_0,r)=(1-t)I_d$ (the $\gamma(t)Z$ term is independent of $X_0$), so the
standard Gaussian conditional-mean formula gives
$\E[X_0\mid r]=\Cov(X_0,r)\,\Var(r)^{-1}r=\tfrac{1-t}{s_t^2}r$; identically,
$\Cov(Z,r)=\gamma(t)I_d$ gives $\E[Z\mid r]=\tfrac{\gamma(t)}{s_t^2}r$. Hence
\[
v_\sigma^\star(x,t,y^i)=\E[\dot X_t\mid X_t=x,t,I=i]
=x^i-\E[X_0\mid r]+\dot\gamma(t)\,\E[Z\mid r]
=x^i+\frac{-(1-t)+\dot\gamma(t)\gamma(t)}{s_t^2}\,r.
\]
Since $\gamma(t)^2=\sigma^2t(1-t)$, differentiating gives
$2\dot\gamma\gamma=\sigma^2(1-2t)$, i.e.\ $\dot\gamma\gamma=\tfrac{\sigma^2}2(1-2t)$,
which yields (a)'s expression for $c(t)$. For the logarithmic-derivative identity,
$\frac{d}{dt}s_t^2=-2(1-t)+2\dot\gamma\gamma=2s_t^2c(t)$ by the same computation, so
$c(t)=\tfrac12\frac{d}{dt}\log s_t^2$.

(b) Along the flow, $u_t:=x_t-tx^i$ satisfies $\dot u_t=\dot x_t-x^i
=v_\sigma^\star(x_t,t,y^i)-x^i=c(t)u_t$ by (a) (with $r=u_t$ evaluated on the
trajectory), a linear ODE with solution
$u_t=u_0\exp\!\big(\int_0^tc(\tau)\,d\tau\big)
=x_0\exp\!\big(\tfrac12[\log s_t^2-\log s_0^2]\big)=x_0\,s_t$, using $s_0=1$ and
$u_0=x_0-0\cdot x^i=x_0$. Hence $x_t=tx^i+s_tx_0$.

(c) Since $s_1=(1-1)[(1-1)+\sigma^2\cdot1]=0$, (b) gives $x_1=x^i$ for every
$x_0\sim\pi_0$ and every $\sigma\ge0$, so $p_1^{\mathrm{cond}}(\cdot\mid y^i)=\delta_{x^i}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[What interpolant noise does and does not change]
\label{cor:prop18}
Interpolant noise does not change the population endpoint law
\eqref{eq:invariance} for any $\sigma$; it \emph{does} raise the irreducible error
($\inf_v L_\sigma>0$ for $\sigma>0$, whereas $\inf_v L_0=0$) and halves the Lipschitz
blow-up rate: $\lim_{t\uparrow1}(1-t)|c(t)|=1$ for $\sigma=0$ and $\tfrac12$ for
$\sigma>0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Endpoint invariance} is Proposition~\ref{prop:prop17}(c).

\emph{Irreducible error.} For $\sigma=0$, $\gamma\equiv0$ so $\dot X_t=X_1-X_0$ is
exactly the target of Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}, giving $\inf_vL_0=0$. For
$\sigma>0$: by the same projection argument as Proposition~\ref{prop:projection},
applied to $\dot X_t$ and $(X_t,t,Y)$, it suffices to show
$\Var(\dot X_t\mid X_t=x,t,I=i)>0$ for some positive-measure set of $t$. Writing
$\dot X_t-x^i=-X_0+\dot\gamma(t)Z$ and $r=(1-t)X_0+\gamma(t)Z$ as in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:prop17}, the standard Gaussian conditioning formula gives,
for $u=(-1,\dot\gamma(t))$ and $w=(1-t,\gamma(t))$,
\[
\Var(\dot X_t\mid r,I=i)=|u|^2-\frac{(u\cdot w)^2}{|w|^2}
=\big(1+\dot\gamma(t)^2\big)-c(t)^2s_t^2,
\]
using $u\cdot w=-(1-t)+\dot\gamma(t)\gamma(t)=c(t)s_t^2$ from the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:prop17}. This vanishes only if $u\parallel w$, i.e.\
$\dot\gamma(t)(1-t)=-\gamma(t)$; substituting
$\gamma(t)=\sigma\sqrt{t(1-t)}$ and $\dot\gamma(t)=\sigma(1-2t)/(2\sqrt{t(1-t)})$
reduces this to $1-2t=-2t$, i.e.\ $1=0$---impossible. So for $\sigma>0$ the
residual variance is strictly positive for \emph{every} $t\in(0,1)$, and
$\inf_vL_\sigma(v)=\E[\Var(\dot X_t\mid X_t,t,Y)]>0$.

\emph{Lipschitz rate.} From (a),
$(1-t)c(t)=\dfrac{-(1-t)+\frac{\sigma^2}2(1-2t)}{(1-t)+\sigma^2t}$, which as
$t\uparrow1$ tends to $-1$ if $\sigma=0$ and to $-\sigma^2/2\;/\;\sigma^2=-\tfrac12$
if $\sigma>0$, giving the stated limits.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The two mechanisms act on different factors]
\label{prop:factors}
In the factorisation \eqref{eq:kernel-field}, label smoothing modifies the label
factor $K_h(y-y^i)$ and therefore changes $p_1$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint});
interpolant noise rescales the spatial factor $\N(tx^i,s_t^2 I_d)$
\emph{isotropically and identically for every $i$}, leaving the endpoint law
unchanged (Proposition~\ref{prop:prop17}). The two remedies are therefore not
interchangeable.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint}, for $h>0$ (and $\sigma=0$) the endpoint law
$p_h^{\mathrm{gen}}(\cdot\mid y)=\sum_ip_i^{(h)}(y)\delta_{x^i}$ depends on $h$
only through the label factor $K_h(y-y^i)$ in \eqref{eq:kernel-field}, and
Corollaries~\ref{cor:zerobw}--\ref{cor:infbw} show it genuinely interpolates
between two distinct endpoint laws as $h$ ranges over $[0,\infty)$. By contrast,
for $h=0$ with distinct labels and any $\sigma\ge0$, $Y=y^i$ already pins $I=i$
deterministically, so no index-posterior argument is needed: Proposition~\ref{prop:prop17}
shows directly that although the spatial factor of Lemma~\ref{lem:cov},
$\N(tx^i,(1-t)^2I_d)$, is replaced by $\N(tx^i,s_t^2I_d)$ for \emph{every} $i$
identically, the endpoint is exactly invariant, $p_1^{\mathrm{cond}}(\cdot\mid y^i)
=\delta_{x^i}$ for every $\sigma$. Since perturbing the label factor changes $p_1$
while perturbing the spatial factor identically across $i$ does not, the two
mechanisms act through different, non-interchangeable channels.
\end{proof}

This cleanly separates the present conditional mechanism from the unconditional
``resample-$x_0$'' remedy of the gradient-variance line \citep{gradvar2025}, which
operates through attainability rather than through the population optimum.

\subsection{Finite capacity: a genuine representation floor}
\label{sec:theory-floor}

The results so far describe the population minimiser over \emph{all}
square-integrable fields; a trained network is restricted to a specific,
finite-capacity class. We now give one concrete way this restriction alone forces a
positive loss floor, independent of any optimisation difficulty.

\begin{proposition}[Lipschitz lower bound]
\label{prop:liplb}
Fix $i$, $t<1$, $f_i(x,t)=(x^i-x)/(1-t)$, and suppose $v(\cdot,t,y^i)$ is
$L$-Lipschitz in $x$. Under $X_t\mid I=i\sim\N(tx^i,(1-t)^2 I_d)$,
$\E[|v(X_t,t,y^i)-f_i(X_t,t)|^2\mid I=i]\ge d\,[1-L(1-t)]_+^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Loss floor under a uniform Lipschitz constraint]
\label{cor:floor}
For $\mathcal F_L=\{v:\operatorname{Lip}_x v(\cdot,t,y^i)\le L\ \forall t,i\}$ with
$L\ge1$ and $t\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)$,
\begin{equation}
\inf_{v\in\mathcal F_L}L_0(v)\ \ge\ d\int_{1-1/L}^1[1-L(1-t)]^2\,dt\ =\ \frac{d}{3L}\ >\ 0.
\label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

Two warnings temper \eqref{eq:floor}. First, $L_{\mathrm{model}}^\star>0$ requires the
constraint: an MLP with unbounded weights lies in no fixed $\mathcal F_L$, and by
universal approximation $\inf_\theta L(v_\theta)$ may be $0$. The correct statement is
conditional: any architecture with $\operatorname{Lip}_x v_\theta\le L$ cannot beat
$d/(3L)$. Second, in our experiments ($d=2$, plateau $L_{\mathrm{trained}}\approx0.48$
at the $2\times10^5$-iteration checkpoint used for the Lipschitz measurement below)
the bound demands only $L\ge1.39$, whereas a trained network has
$\operatorname{Lip}_x$ of order $10^1$--$10^2$, giving a floor of order $10^{-2}$---one
to two orders of magnitude below the plateau. Corollary~\ref{cor:floor} is thus best
used as a \emph{measurement}: estimate $L$, compare $d/(3L)$ to $L_{\mathrm{trained}}$,
and report which gap dominates (Section~\ref{sec:aux}).

\subsection{Finite-sample memorisation and exact non-representability}
\label{sec:theory-d2}

This subsection translates two representability results from the unconditional
gradient-variance/stochastic-interpolant literature \citep{gradvar2025} into the
present notation: (i)~why a fixed finite \emph{unconditional} batch already admits a
zero-loss memorising field despite Proposition~\ref{prop:uncond}'s positive
population loss, and (ii)~why no finite network can exactly represent the collapsed
field $(\star)$. Unlike Sections~\ref{sec:theory-hard}--\ref{sec:theory-floor}, the
finite sample itself is treated as random here.

\begin{lemma}[Generic non-intersection of finite interpolant segments]
\label{lem:nonintersect}
Let $x_0^1,\dots,x_0^N\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\pi_0$ and, independently,
$z^1,\dots,z^N\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\rho_1$ be $N$ unconditional
source--target pairs (no labels), both laws absolutely continuous on $\R^d$, and
$\ell_i(t)=(1-t)x_0^i+tz^i$, $t\in[0,1]$.
(a) If $d\ge2$: for any fixed $i\ne j$,
$\Pr[\exists\,t\in(0,1):\ell_i(t)=\ell_j(t)]=0$.
(b) If $d>2$: for any fixed $i\ne j$,
$\Pr[\exists\,t_i,t_j\in(0,1),\,t_i\ne t_j:\ell_i(t_i)=\ell_j(t_j)]=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) Fix $\hat t\in(0,1)$; $\ell_i(\hat t)=\ell_j(\hat t)$ solves to
$z^j=\tfrac{1-\hat t}{\hat t}(x_0^i-x_0^j)+z^i$, which pins $z^j$ to a
one-dimensional affine subspace of $\R^d$ determined by $(x_0^i,x_0^j,z^i,\hat t)$.
This subspace is Lebesgue-null whenever $d\ge2$, and $\rho_1$ is absolutely
continuous, so conditionally on $(x_0^i,x_0^j,z^i)$ the event has probability $0$;
integrating over $(x_0^i,x_0^j,z^i)$ preserves probability $0$, and the bound is
uniform in $\hat t$, so the same holds for "some $t=\hat t$" (the constraint is on
$z^j$ alone and does not depend on which $\hat t$ occurs). A union bound over the
finitely many pairs $(i,j)$ finishes (a). (b) With $t_i\ne t_j$ both free, solving
for $z^j$ pins it to a two-dimensional affine subspace, Lebesgue-null when $d>2$;
the rest of the argument is identical.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A memorising field always exists on a fixed finite unconditional batch]
\label{prop:memorize}
Fix $N$ unconditional pairs $\{(x_0^i,z^i)\}_{i=1}^N$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:nonintersect}
with $d>2$ and the $z^i$ pairwise distinct a.s., and sample $m$ time points
$t^{(i,j)}\in(0,1)$, $j=1,\dots,m$, independently across $(i,j)$. Let
$X_t^{(i,j)}=(1-t^{(i,j)})x_0^i+t^{(i,j)}z^i$ and
\[
L_{\mathrm{MC}}^{\mathrm{unc}}(v)=\frac1{Nm}\sum_{i=1}^N\sum_{j=1}^m
\big|(z^i-x_0^i)-v\big(X_t^{(i,j)},t^{(i,j)}\big)\big|^2.
\]
Then, almost surely over the draw of $\{(x_0^i,z^i)\}$ and $\{t^{(i,j)}\}$, some
$v$ attains $L_{\mathrm{MC}}^{\mathrm{unc}}(v)=0$---even though
$\inf_v L_{\mathrm{unc}}(v)>0$ at the population level whenever the $z^i$ are not
all equal (Proposition~\ref{prop:uncond}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:nonintersect}(a)--(b), union-bounded over the $\binom N2$ pairs,
almost surely no two of the $N$ full segments $\{\ell_i(t):t\in(0,1)\}$ intersect at
all, so in particular the $Nm$ sampled points $(X_t^{(i,j)},t^{(i,j)})$ never
collide across different $i$. On this full-measure event $(z,t)\mapsto i$ is well
defined on the sample, so setting
$v(z,t):=z^{i(z,t)}-x_0^{i(z,t)}$ on the sample and $v:=0$ elsewhere gives
$v(X_t^{(i,j)},t^{(i,j)})=z^i-x_0^i$ exactly for every sampled $(i,j)$, so every
summand of $L_{\mathrm{MC}}^{\mathrm{unc}}$ vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why SGD does not land here]
\label{rem:resample}
Proposition~\ref{prop:memorize} is valid only for one \emph{fixed} finite sample.
Standard CFM training resamples $x_0^i$ (and the time samples) independently every
epoch, so the lookup table fit at epoch $k$ is checked, at epoch $k+1$, against
points it never saw; no single lookup table stays consistent with a growing stream
of $(x_0,t)$ draws. This is the precise mechanism behind the informal claim that
resampling $x_0$---not mere continuity of $\pi_0$---is what pushes unconditional
training toward the population optimum of Proposition~\ref{prop:uncond} rather than
a per-example memorising solution, and it gives a concrete reading of P6
(Table~\ref{tab:sweeps}): at fixed capacity, larger $N$ makes the epoch-consistent
lookup table harder to realise with a smooth finite-parameter function, pushing the
attained solution back toward the population optimum. Note the asymmetry with
Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}: conditional collapse needs no genericity argument
and no fixed-batch caveat, because $y^i$ pins down $i$ deterministically for every
$x_0$, not merely with probability $1$ on one finite draw---resampling $x_0$ every
epoch does nothing to prevent it, which is exactly why conditional collapse is
universal while unconditional memorisation is not.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[The collapse field cannot be exactly represented by a finite network]
\label{lem:norepr}
Let $g(t)=1/(1-t)$ on $[0,1)$ and let $f_\theta:\R\to\R$ be any finite-depth,
finite-width feedforward network with finite real weights, built from affine layers
and globally Lipschitz activations (this covers ReLU, leaky-ReLU, tanh, sigmoid,
GELU/SiLU, softplus, and every activation used in our experiments). Then
$\sup_{t\in[0,1)}|f_\theta(t)-g(t)|=\infty$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A finite composition of affine maps (finite weight matrices, hence finite operator
norm) and globally Lipschitz activations is globally Lipschitz on $\R$, with
constant equal to the product of the finitely many layer constants. A Lipschitz
function is bounded on any bounded set: fixing $t_0\in[0,1)$,
$|f_\theta(t)|\le|f_\theta(t_0)|+K|t-t_0|\le|f_\theta(t_0)|+K$ for all $t\in[0,1)$,
so $\sup_{t\in[0,1)}|f_\theta(t)|<\infty$. But $g(t)\to\infty$ as $t\uparrow1$, so
$g$ is unbounded on $[0,1)$, hence so is $f_\theta-g$.
\end{proof}

For $x\ne x^i$ fixed, $t\mapsto v_0^\star(x,t,y^i)=(x^i-x)/(1-t)$ has exactly this
$1/(1-t)$ blow-up along the direction $x^i-x$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:norepr} (applied
coordinatewise to that direction) shows that \emph{no} finite network of the kind
used in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} can represent $v_0^\star$ \emph{exactly} on
all of $[0,1)$: for every fixed parameter vector $\theta$ there is some $(x,t)$ at
which $v_\theta\ne v_0^\star$. This does not contradict universal approximation,
which guarantees only \emph{uniform} approximation on \emph{compact} sets: every
$[0,1-\delta]$, $\delta>0$, is disjoint from the singularity at $t=1$---exactly the
regime the sampler must traverse to reach the collapse point $x^i$, and exactly
where the ODE solver's $t=1$ handling matters in practice. We stop short, however,
of claiming this pointwise non-representability forces
$L^\star_{\mathrm{model}}-L^\star_{\mathrm{all}}>0$ for an architecture whose
weights (hence Lipschitz constant) are unconstrained: pointwise blow-up on a
shrinking neighbourhood of $t=1$ is compatible, in principle, with a sequence of
increasingly ill-conditioned finite networks driving the \emph{expected} loss
$L_0(v_\theta)$ arbitrarily close to $0$ (consistent with the growing empirical
Lipschitz constant we measure in Figure~\ref{fig:lipschitz}). The genuine,
quantitative loss floor we prove, $d/(3L)$, therefore applies only to the
Lipschitz-capped class $\mathcal F_L$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:floor}, and should not
be read as a statement about arbitrary finite architectures.

\subsection{Scope: what is and is not predicted}
\label{sec:scope}

The population theory proves exact consequences for the empirical minimiser
(Propositions~\ref{prop:collapse}--\ref{prop:nwrate}, Corollaries~\ref{cor:zerobw}
and~\ref{cor:infbw}, Proposition~\ref{prop:liplb}, Corollary~\ref{cor:floor}).
Section~\ref{sec:theory-d2} additionally proves exact statements about a
\emph{randomly drawn} finite unconditional batch (Lemma~\ref{lem:nonintersect},
Proposition~\ref{prop:memorize}, Lemma~\ref{lem:norepr})---these are not part of the
fixed-dataset empirical-population characterisation above, but are exact
nonetheless. It does \emph{not} determine: the number of SGD steps to approach the optimum; the
dependence of \emph{observed} collapse on $N$ at fixed architecture (the population
optimum collapses for every finite $N$, so a non-collapsing $N{=}5000$ run probes
representation/optimisation, it does not contradict Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse});
the dependence of observed collapse on $\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}$ (the theory implies no
monotonicity, so a flat sweep is \emph{consistent with}, not a falsification of, the
theory); the monotonicity of $\tr\Covh$ in $h$; or finite-sample error bars.

\begin{quote}\small\itshape
Phrasing rule. A quantity in the second list is never a ``theoretical prediction''
that ``matched'' or ``failed.'' Correct form: the exact population theory does not
determine $X$; the observed behaviour of $X$ is an empirical finding. We follow this
rule in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, marking such results ``(empirical).''
\end{quote}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.} Unless noted, EXP-1 uses the linear-Gaussian problem
$d{=}2,\,k{=}1,\,N{=}200,\,\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}{=}0.1$, prior and source $\N(0,I)$,
deterministic linear interpolant ($\sigma{=}0$); a 4-layer width-128 MLP with SiLU and
a sinusoidal time embedding (dim 64); Adam at $\text{lr}{=}10^{-3}$, batch 256; RK4
integration with 100 steps stopping at $t{=}1{-}10^{-3}$; evaluation over $M{=}1000$
samples at 20 training conditions and 8 held-out conditions. We do \emph{not}
early-stop: overtraining is the object of study. Sanity checks pass first (analytic
posterior satisfies the normal equations to $1.8\times10^{-15}$; closed-form field
integration maps every $x_0\to x^i$; the unconditional model does not leak $y$), so
the collapse below is not an artefact of a $y$-leak or a broken ODE.
Parameter sweeps use five seeds per point (seeds 0--2 on CPU, seeds 3--4 on
$2\times$T4 GPU via the same training code, \texttt{device: auto}). The seed
controls \emph{both} the random draw of the problem instance (the forward operator
$A$, hence $\Sigmapost$) \emph{and} all training/data randomness, so the reported
seed-to-seed spread reflects both sources jointly rather than SGD noise at a fixed
problem alone; we did not find it necessary to disentangle the two for the
conclusions drawn here, but flag this as a conflation a reader should be aware of
when interpreting the error bars below.

The variance tracks the true posterior up to $\sim\!10^4$ iterations---the model is a
correct Bayesian sampler in this phase, which is exactly why early stopping works
\citep{physicscfm2026}---and then collapses. At $2\times10^5$ the variance has not yet
reached $0$ but stalls near $0.40$. A single-seed extended run at a \emph{fixed}
learning rate shows all four observables decreasing together with the loss (to
$0.16$/$0.36$ at $7\times10^5$), so the residual at $2\times10^5$ is an
\emph{optimisation} gap, not a failure of Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}---but that
fixed-lr run eventually diverges near $10^6$ iterations, since the target $(\star)$
steepens as $t\to1$ and a constant step size becomes too large once the loss is
already small. Adding a cosine learning-rate schedule
($\text{lr}:10^{-3}\to10^{-6}$ over the run) removes this divergence entirely. The
cleanest evidence is a like-for-like, same-seed comparison: at the identical
$7\times10^5$-iteration checkpoint, seed~0 reaches $\tr\Cov=0.128$ with the
schedule versus $0.162$ at a fixed learning rate, and continues to $0.115$ by
$1{\times}10^6$ with no sign of the fixed-lr run's eventual blow-up. Averaged over
\emph{5 seeds} (rerun with the schedule), the mean trajectory decreases from
$\gtrsim1$ to $0.120\pm0.054$ at $1{\times}10^6$ iterations; we report this mean
mainly to show that no seed diverges, not as a tight improvement over the old
single-seed value of $0.16$---the between-seed standard deviation is large enough
(individual seeds at $1{\times}10^6$ range from $0.035$ to $0.186$) that $0.16$ is
not clearly excluded by $0.120\pm0.054$, and this spread is inflated by the
seed-conflated problem instance noted in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} (a harder or
easier random $A$, not only training noise). The improvement is also not uniform
across the last third of training: from $7\times10^5$ to $1\times10^6$---by which
point the learning rate has decayed to $\lesssim2\times10^{-4}$---four of five seeds
improve and one worsens slightly, so this tail mostly protects against divergence
rather than reliably deepening the collapse further. With that said, the memorisation
ratio of the paragraph below reaches $0.955\pm0.020$ at $1\times10^6$---95\% of
generated samples individually satisfy the nearest-neighbour memorisation
criterion, a low-variance and unambiguous confirmation that collapse continues. Taken
together, this resolves the open question of Section~\ref{sec:limitations}: reaching
deep collapse cleanly, without divergence, needs only a decaying learning rate, not a
change to the theory, even though we cannot yet say precisely how much deeper a
better-tuned schedule could push $\tr\Cov$.

\paragraph{A literature-standard memorisation ratio confirms Corollary~\ref{cor:distinction}.}
The diagnostics above are all aggregate (trace of a sample covariance, or distances
of the sample \emph{mean}). As a per-sample complement, we report the
\emph{memorisation ratio} of \citet{yoon2023memorization}\footnote{As used, e.g., by
\citet{buchanan2025edge}.}: a generated sample $\hat x$ is \emph{memorised} if
$\|\hat x-x^{(1)}\|^2\le c\,\|\hat x-x^{(2)}\|^2$ for $c=1/9$, where $x^{(1)},x^{(2)}$
are the nearest and second-nearest training points to $\hat x$. Figure~\ref{fig:memratio}
shows this ratio for the same conditional and unconditional EXP-1 runs as Figure~\ref{fig:p1p4}
(5 seeds each): both rise from a common $\approx0.11$ baseline as training
proceeds, but the conditional ratio reaches $0.650\pm0.033$ at $2\times10^5$
iterations versus $0.389\pm0.032$ unconditionally---direct, literature-standard
confirmation that \emph{both} flows memorise (Corollary~\ref{cor:distinction}), with
conditional memorisation the stronger of the two, exactly as the single-atom vs.\
full-empirical-measure distinction predicts. The two ratios are not directly
comparable in magnitude---the unconditional target is spread over $N{=}200$
candidate atoms rather than one, so the $c=1/9$ dominance criterion is mechanically
harder to satisfy there even at equally exact memorisation---so we read this only
as a directional confirmation (both rise well above baseline; conditional rises
further), not as a calibrated measure of \emph{how much more} conditional
memorisation occurs. On EXP-2 (Section~\ref{sec:exp23}), the
same ratio reaches $0.83\pm0.12$ by $3\times10^5$ iterations (2 seeds), tracking the
mode-coverage collapse there. Pushed further still, the extended
learning-rate-scheduled run of Section~\ref{sec:exp1} reaches
$0.955\pm0.020$ by $10^6$ iterations (5 seeds): 95\% of individual samples are
memorised by this point, not merely the sample mean.

\subsection{Adversarial pairing: collapse does not require a real posterior}
\label{sec:adversarial}

Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} never uses the forward operator $A$ or
$\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}$: it only needs the labels $y^1,\dots,y^N$ to be pairwise
distinct. The theory therefore predicts that collapse is unchanged if $Y$ is
\emph{randomly permuted relative to $X$} before training, i.e.\ if $(x^i,y^i)$ is no
longer the true generative pair---an identification mechanism, not evidence that the
model discovered real posterior structure. This mirrors the ``Adversarial Pairings''
experiment of \citet{gradvar2025} (shuffling the targets of ground-truth OT couplings
on CelebA to test unconditional memorisation), adapted here to the conditional case,
where the prediction is exact rather than a generic-position argument
(cf.\ Remark~\ref{rem:resample}).

\begin{figure}[htb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.68\textwidth]{fig_adv_trace_cov.png}
\caption{$\tr\Cov$ under the real EXP-1 pairing (5 seeds, as in Fig.~\ref{fig:p1p4})
vs.\ the same architecture and schedule trained on $Y$ randomly permuted relative to
$X$ (5 seeds). Both collapse monotonically well below $\tr\Sigmapost$; the shuffled
run lags the real pairing at $2\times10^5$ iterations ($0.625\pm0.108$ vs.\
$0.396\pm0.127$)---an optimisation-gap difference, not a failure of
Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}, whose population target is $0$ either way.}
\label{fig:adv-trace}
\end{figure}

We re-run the EXP-1 architecture and training schedule unchanged (5 seeds), permuting
$Y$ with an independent random permutation per seed before training
(configs/exp1\_adversarial\_shuffle.yaml). Figure~\ref{fig:adv-trace} confirms the
qualitative prediction: $\tr\Cov$ collapses under shuffled labels just as it does
under the real pairing, from $\gtrsim1$ down to $0.625\pm0.108$ at $2\times10^5$
iterations (vs.\ $0.396\pm0.127$ for the real pairing)---the gap is an
optimisation-difficulty difference at a fixed finite iteration budget (a random $x$-$y$
pairing is a harder target function to fit than the smooth linear-Gaussian one), not a
difference in the population optimum, which Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} fixes at
$0$ regardless of how $Y$ was assigned.

\begin{figure}[htb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.68\textwidth]{fig_adv_mean_targets.png}
\caption{Under shuffled labels, the generated mean converges to the \emph{assigned}
training point $x^i$ ($\|\text{mean}-x^i\|:1.35\pm0.10\to0.559\pm0.104$) while moving
\emph{away} from the true analytic posterior mean of that $y^i$
($\|\text{mean}-\mu_{\mathrm{post}}(y^i)\|:0.84\pm0.13\to1.437\pm0.176$)---the two
curves cross between $10^4$ and $3\times10^4$ iterations. Under the real pairing the
corresponding gap is far smaller ($\|\text{mean}-\mu_{\mathrm{post}}\|=0.656\pm0.177$
at $2\times10^5$, Table~\ref{tab:exp1}), since there $x^i$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{post}}(y^i)$
are close by construction.}
\label{fig:adv-mean}
\end{figure}

The sharper diagnostic is Figure~\ref{fig:adv-mean}: $\|\text{mean}-\mu_{\mathrm{post}}(y^i)\|$,
the distance to $y^i$'s \emph{true} analytic posterior mean (still well defined and
computable from $A$---it is simply now unrelated to the $x^i$ the label was
shuffled onto), does not shrink; it \emph{grows} throughout training, crossing above
$\|\text{mean}-x^i\|$ by $10^5$ iterations. The generated law tracks the arbitrary
label-to-example assignment, not any statistical relationship between $x$ and $y$---the
same conclusion Corollary~\ref{cor:distinction} draws from the exact theory, now
confirmed even when the labels carry no real information about $x$ at all.

\subsection{Additional verification (gap to optimum, Lipschitz, posterior distance)}

Three checkpoint-only measurements corroborate the theory.
(i)~\emph{Optimality gap.} The Monte-Carlo gap $L(v_\theta)-L(\vstar)$, with
$L(\vstar)=\E[\Var(U\mid X_t,t,\widetilde Y)]$ computed from the exact minimiser, is

(e.g.\ $h{=}0.1$: $1.14\to0.22\to0.102$ at $10^2/3{\times}10^4/2{\times}10^5$ steps),

\subsection{EXP-2 and EXP-3: selective memorisation beyond the Gaussian case}
\label{sec:exp23}

\paragraph{EXP-2 (Gaussian mixture).} A 4-mode GMM prior with a rank-deflating linear
observation gives a genuinely \emph{bimodal} analytic posterior. As the loss descends
(Fig.~\ref{fig:exp2curves}, 2 seeds), mode coverage falls $1.00\to0.72$ (toward $0.5$,
i.e.\ one of the two modes) and the MMD to the true posterior grows from $\sim0.04$ to
$\sim0.25$; up to $\sim\!10^4$ iterations coverage is a full $1.0$ and the MMD is near
zero---the model is a calibrated posterior sampler---before overtraining drives it onto
$x^i$ in a single mode and abandons the other. This is selective memorisation, and its
onset at $\sim\!10^4$ iterations mirrors EXP-1.

\paragraph{EXP-3 (MNIST inpainting).} Masking the lower half of $32\times32$ MNIST
($N{=}500$) with a small U-Net, we draw 16 completions per condition. Over \emph{3
seeds}, the inpainted-region pixel variance drops $\sim100\times$ by iteration
$15000$ ($0.137\pm0.021\to(1.17\pm0.12)\times10^{-3}$) while the distance to the
nearest training image falls to $(0.77\pm0.26)\times10^{-3}$ and the observed-region
reconstruction stays small, $(1.7\pm1.3)\times10^{-4}$ (larger and noisier across
seeds than the single-seed value reported in an earlier draft of this
experiment, $1.9\times10^{-5}$, but still three orders of magnitude below the
pixel-variance collapse). Qualitatively, diverse completions at iteration 200 sharpen
progressively and become identical by iteration 15000, coinciding with the nearest
training image (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp3prog})---the same collapse on real image data, reached
earlier because the loss converges deeper here.

\paragraph{A harder dataset: CIFAR-10 inpainting.} To check this is not an
artefact of MNIST's simplicity, we repeat EXP-3 verbatim (same architecture,
$N{=}500$, $15000$ iterations) on CIFAR-10---natural RGB images with far more
texture and per-pixel variability than handwritten digits (one seed; see
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The same collapse occurs: inpainted-region pixel
variance falls from $0.142$ to $0.0087$ ($\sim16\times$) and the nearest-training-image
distance falls from $0.057$ to $0.0040$, while completions visibly sharpen from
diverse noisy blobs at iteration $200$ to samples indistinguishable from each
other and from the memorised training image by iteration $15000$
(Figure~\ref{fig:exp3cifar}).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The theory does not quantitatively explain the residual optimisation gap.}
A finite network trained by SGD approaches the population minimiser only up to an
optimisation gap, which is why hard-conditioning collapse is
\emph{optimisation-paced} and only partial within a finite budget (for $h>0$ the
model does reach the population optimum $\Covh$). Corollary~\ref{cor:floor}'s
Lipschitz-capped representation floor was our candidate explanation for why the
$h=0$ plateau stalls above zero, but Section~\ref{sec:aux} shows it does
\emph{not} bind here---the floor is $\sim\!50\times$ below the observed plateau. At
a \emph{fixed} learning rate, extreme overtraining eventually diverges near
$10^6$ iterations, since the collapsed target's Lipschitz constant grows as
$t\to1$ and a constant step size becomes too large once the loss is small; a
cosine learning-rate decay removes this divergence and lets training continue
collapsing further without blowing up (Table~\ref{tab:exp1},
Figure~\ref{fig:extended}), confirming that the plateau is a \emph{transient},
schedule-sensitive optimisation phenomenon rather
than a hard floor. We therefore cannot currently say \emph{why} training stalls at
the specific rate it does at a fixed learning rate, or predict the schedule that
minimises iterations-to-collapse; characterising this optimisation gap
quantitatively---as opposed to merely bounding it away from a representation
explanation and showing empirically that it is schedule-sensitive---remains an open
problem this paper does not solve.

\paragraph{All experiments are small-scale.} EXP-1/EXP-2 are $d\le10$ synthetic
linear-Gaussian/Gaussian-mixture problems, and EXP-3 (now 3 seeds on MNIST, plus one
seed on CIFAR-10 as a harder, more textured dataset; Section~\ref{sec:exp23}) still
uses only $32\times32$ images, $N{=}500$, and one small U-Net---we do not validate
the theory on larger images, other modalities, or the U-Net/transformer backbones
used in production diffusion and flow models. We expect the population-level
mechanism to persist at scale (it is architecture-agnostic by construction, since
Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} constrains only the population minimiser, not any
particular parameterisation), and the CIFAR-10 replication is one data point in that
direction, but a systematic study across resolutions, modalities, and production-scale
backbones remains future work; so does a mask-type and $N$ sweep for EXP-3, and
extending CIFAR-10 to multiple seeds.

\paragraph{Limited architecture and optimiser sensitivity study.} All headline
EXP-1/EXP-2 results use one fixed architecture (4-layer, width-128 MLP with SiLU)
and optimiser (Adam, $\text{lr}=10^{-3}$). Section~\ref{sec:exp1} reports a small
width/depth/optimiser sweep (2 seeds per configuration) confirming that collapse is
not an artefact of this specific choice, but that sweep is limited to $\tr\Cov$ at a
single iteration budget on EXP-1; we do not study how the empirical Lipschitz
constant of Section~\ref{sec:aux} depends on width or depth (Section~\ref{sec:theory-floor}'s
representation-floor argument is sensitive to exactly this), nor do we repeat the
sweep on EXP-2 or EXP-3, nor sweep the optimiser's learning rate or the SGD
momentum coefficient.

\paragraph{The paper is diagnostic, not prescriptive.} We characterise why
collapse happens and why two natural remedies (label smoothing, interpolant noise)
do or do not work, but we do not propose or evaluate a working fix: the atomicity
obstruction (Proposition~\ref{prop:atomicity}) means the label-smoothing
``remedy'' matches moments but never recovers a continuous posterior, and we offer
no alternative that does.

\paragraph{Other caveats.} The sweeps P5 and P6 are empirical findings the
population theory does not determine and must not be read as prediction matches.
The random seed used in EXP-1/EXP-2 controls both the problem instance and the
training run jointly (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). Checkpoints are regenerable
and not tracked in version control, so reproducing the checkpoint-based tables from
a clean clone requires retraining the $p7y$ runs (deterministic per seed); all
sweep tables reproduce directly from the tracked metrics.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Conditional collapse in flow matching is the zero-bandwidth limit of a kernel-weighted
empirical conditional flow. Under hard conditioning with identifying labels the exact
population minimiser has zero conditional variance; label smoothing turns this into
exact kernel regression over the training atoms, with a parameter-free moment reference
that a trained network tracks to within seed noise across five seeds. The two apparent
remedies are provably different---label noise moves the population endpoint, interpolant
noise cannot---and even the working remedy only matches moments, bounded away from the
true posterior by an $h$-independent Wasserstein floor. The same collapse holds on a
bimodal GMM posterior, on MNIST and CIFAR-10 inpainting, across a range of MLP
widths/depths and both Adam and SGD, and---confirmed by a literature-standard
memorisation ratio---even when the training labels are shuffled and carry no real
information about $x$ at all, exactly as the identification mechanism predicts.

\section{Deferred proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\begin{lemma}[Change of variables]
\label{lem:cov}
Fix $i$ and $t<1$. The map $X_0\mapsto X_t=(1-t)X_0+tx^i$ is invertible affine with
$X_0=(X_t-tx^i)/(1-t)$, so $\sigma(X_t\mid I=i)=\sigma(X_0\mid I=i)$ and the conditional
density is $p(x\mid I=i,t)=(1-t)^{-d}\pi_0\!\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)$. The Jacobian
$(1-t)^{-d}$ is $i$-independent and cancels in every posterior over $I$. Because $\pi_0$
is Gaussian, $p(x\mid I=i,t)>0$ for every $x$ and $t<1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Well-posedness]
\label{lem:wellposed}
Let $w_1,\dots,w_N:\R^d\times[0,1)\to[0,1]$ be $C^\infty$ with $\sum_i w_i\equiv1$ and
$v(x,t)=\sum_i w_i(x,t)(x^i-x)/(1-t)$. Then $v$ is $C^\infty$ hence locally Lipschitz on
$\R^d\times[0,1)$, the ODE $\dot x_t=v(x_t,t)$ has a unique solution on $[0,1)$ for every
initial condition, and with $M=\max_i|x^i|$, $|x_t|+M\le(|x_0|+M)/(1-t)$. Consequently
the continuity equation has a unique measure solution and endpoint laws are the weak
limits $p_1=\lim_{t\uparrow1}p_t$.
\end{lemma}

Both $v_{\mathrm{unc}}^\star$ and $\vstar$ have this form with smooth weights, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:wellposed} applies and no ``assume the flow is well defined'' hypothesis
is needed. We now give the remaining proofs in full.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:uncond}]
Equation~\eqref{eq:distinction}'s $q_i(x,t)$ is Bayes' rule applied to
Lemma~\ref{lem:cov}'s density $p(x\mid I=i,t)=(1-t)^{-d}\pi_0\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)$
under the uniform prior on $I$: the Jacobian $(1-t)^{-d}$ and the prior $1/N$ do not
depend on $i$ and cancel, leaving $q_i(x,t)\propto\pi_0\big(\tfrac{x-tx^i}{1-t}\big)$.
Given $I=i$, Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}(a)'s argument gives
$U=(x^i-x)/(1-t)$; averaging over the posterior $q_i(x,t)$ and substituting into
Proposition~\ref{prop:projection} gives $v_{\mathrm{unc}}^\star$. For the endpoint
law: by construction $v_{\mathrm{unc}}^\star(x,t)=\E[\dot X_t\mid X_t=x,t]$, which is
exactly the velocity field appearing in the continuity equation for the marginal law
of the interpolation. By Lemma~\ref{lem:wellposed} the flow is well posed on $[0,1)$
and transports $p_0=\pi_0$ to $p_t=\mathrm{Law}(X_t)$ for every $t<1$; since
$X_t\to X_1\sim\rhohat_X$ almost surely, $p_t\Rightarrow\rhohat_X$, giving
$p_1^{\mathrm{unc}}=\rhohat_X$.

For $\inf_v L_{\mathrm{unc}}>0$: fix $t<1$ and $x$. By the remark after
Lemma~\ref{lem:cov}, $\pi_0$ is everywhere positive, so $q_i(x,t)>0$ for every $i$.
Conditionally on $(X_t=x,t,I=i)$ the target is the deterministic vector
$u_i=(x^i-x)/(1-t)$, and $u_i\ne u_j$ whenever $x^i\ne x^j$. Hence the discrete law
$\{q_i(x,t)\}_i$ places positive mass on at least two distinct values $u_i\ne u_j$
whenever some $x^i\ne x^j$, so
$\Var(U\mid X_t=x,t)=\sum_i q_i(x,t)\,|u_i-\bar u(x,t)|^2>0$
at \emph{every} $(x,t)$ with $t<1$, where $\bar u(x,t)=\sum_i q_i(x,t)u_i$.
Integrating over $(x,t)$ and applying Proposition~\ref{prop:projection},
$\inf_v L_{\mathrm{unc}}(v)=\E[\Var(U\mid X_t,t)]>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:expansion}]
Throughout, $\rho_Y$ denotes the marginal density of $Y$ and $s(y):=\nabla\log\rho_Y(y)\in\R^k$
its score at $y$, both well defined since $\rho_Y$ is positive and $C^2$ near $y$.

\emph{Step 0: an exact identity via a tilted population measure.} Define the
$h$-tilted density of $Y$,
\[
q_h(y'):=\frac{K_h(y-y')\,\rho_Y(y')}{\int K_h(y-y'')\,\rho_Y(y'')\,dy''},
\]
and, for a measurable $f$, $\E_h[f(X,Y)]:=\E[K_h(y-Y)f(X,Y)]/\E[K_h(y-Y)]$, i.e.\
expectation under $Y\sim q_h$ with $X\mid Y$ unchanged. As $N\to\infty$ the empirical
kernel-weighted moments $\bar x_h(y),\Covh(y)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}
converge to their population counterparts $\E_h[X]$, $\Cov_h^{\mathrm{pop}}(y):=\Cov_h[X]$
(a law of large numbers for the weighted sums $\sum_jK_h(y-y^j)x^j$ and
$\sum_jK_h(y-y^j)$ separately, then Slutsky's theorem for the ratio); the discrepancy
between the finite-$N$ empirical moments and this population limit is the
$O_P((Nh^k)^{-1/2})$ term addressed in Step~3. It therefore suffices to show
\begin{equation}
\Cov_h^{\mathrm{pop}}(y)=\Sigma(y)+h^2J(y)J(y)^\top+O(h^4).
\label{eq:expansion-pop}
\end{equation}
By the law of total covariance applied to $X$ given $Y$ under the tilted law $Y\sim q_h$,
\begin{equation}
\Cov_h^{\mathrm{pop}}(y)=\underbrace{\E_h[\Sigma(Y)]}_{\text{within-group}}
+\underbrace{\Cov_h[\mu(Y)]}_{\text{between-group}},
\label{eq:expansion-totcov}
\end{equation}
which is an exact identity for every $h>0$, since $\Sigma(Y)=\Cov(X\mid Y)$ and
$\mu(Y)=\E[X\mid Y]$ by definition and the law of total covariance requires no
approximation.

\emph{Step 1: the tilted law of $Y$, rescaled.} Substitute $y'=y+hu$
($dy'=h^k\,du$) and write $K_h(y-y')=h^{-k}\varphi(u)$ with
$\varphi(u)=(2\pi)^{-k/2}e^{-|u|^2/2}$ the standard Gaussian density (immediate from
the definition of $K_h$). Then
\[
\int K_h(y-y'')\rho_Y(y'')\,dy''=\int\varphi(u)\rho_Y(y+hu)\,du,
\]
and by the $C^2$ Taylor expansion $\rho_Y(y+hu)=\rho_Y(y)\big[1+h\,s(y)^\top u+O(h^2)\big]$
(uniformly over the region where $\varphi$ has non-negligible mass, using $C^2$
regularity of $\rho_Y$ near $y$), together with $\int\varphi(u)u\,du=0$,
\[
\int\varphi(u)\rho_Y(y+hu)\,du=\rho_Y(y)\big[1+O(h^2)\big].
\]
Hence the tilted law of $u:=(Y-y)/h$ under $q_h$ has density
\begin{equation}
\tilde q_h(u)=\frac{\varphi(u)\rho_Y(y+hu)}{\int\varphi(u')\rho_Y(y+hu')\,du'}
=\varphi(u)\big[1+h\,s(y)^\top u+O(h^2)\big].
\label{eq:tilted-u}
\end{equation}
Using $\E_\varphi[u]=0$, $\E_\varphi[uu^\top]=I_k$, $\E_\varphi[u_au_bu_c]=0$ (odd
moments of a centred Gaussian vanish) and $\E_\varphi[u_au_bu_cu_d]=\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd}
+\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}+\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}$, \eqref{eq:tilted-u} gives the moments
\begin{align}
\E_{\tilde q_h}[u]&=\E_\varphi[u]+h\,\E_\varphi\!\big[u\,(s^\top u)\big]+O(h^2)
=h\,\E_\varphi[uu^\top]s+O(h^2)=h\,s(y)+O(h^2),
\label{eq:u-mean}\\
\E_{\tilde q_h}[uu^\top]&=\E_\varphi[uu^\top]+h\,\E_\varphi\!\big[uu^\top(s^\top u)\big]+O(h^2)
=I_k+0+O(h^2),
\label{eq:u-2mom}
\end{align}
the vanishing middle term in \eqref{eq:u-2mom} because $\E_\varphi[u_au_bu_c]=0$ for
every $a,b,c$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
\Cov_{\tilde q_h}[u]=\E_{\tilde q_h}[uu^\top]-\E_{\tilde q_h}[u]\E_{\tilde q_h}[u]^\top
=I_k+O(h^2)-h^2s(y)s(y)^\top=I_k+O(h^2).
\label{eq:u-cov}
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 2: within-group term.} By $C^1$ smoothness of $\Sigma(\cdot)$,
$\Sigma(y+hu)=\Sigma(y)+h\,[\nabla\Sigma(y)\cdot u]+O(h^2)$ (a matrix-valued
first-order expansion, contracting the gradient 3-tensor with $u$), so by
\eqref{eq:tilted-u} and \eqref{eq:u-mean},
\[
\E_h[\Sigma(Y)]=\E_{\tilde q_h}[\Sigma(y+hu)]
=\Sigma(y)+h\,\big[\nabla\Sigma(y)\cdot\E_{\tilde q_h}[u]\big]+O(h^2)
=\Sigma(y)+O(h^2),
\]
since $\E_{\tilde q_h}[u]=O(h)$ by \eqref{eq:u-mean}, making the second term $O(h^2)$
outright. This is the within-group term of \eqref{eq:expansion}.

\emph{Step 3: between-group term.} By the $C^2$ Taylor expansion of $\mu$,
\[
\mu(y+hu)=\mu(y)+hJ(y)u+\tfrac{h^2}{2}Q(u)+O(h^3),\qquad
Q(u)_a:=\sum_{b,c}\partial^2_{y_by_c}\mu_a(y)\,u_bu_c,
\]
and since covariance is invariant to the additive constant $\mu(y)$,
\begin{equation}
\Cov_h[\mu(Y)]=\Cov_{\tilde q_h}\!\Big[hJ(y)u+\tfrac{h^2}2Q(u)+O(h^3)\Big]
=h^2J(y)\Cov_{\tilde q_h}[u]J(y)^\top
+h^3\Big[J(y)\Cov_{\tilde q_h}(u,Q(u))+\mathrm{sym}\Big]
+h^4\Cov_{\tilde q_h}[Q(u)].
\label{eq:expansion-terms}
\end{equation}
The leading term is $h^2J(y)\big[I_k+O(h^2)\big]J(y)^\top=h^2J(y)J(y)^\top+O(h^4)$ by
\eqref{eq:u-cov}. It remains to show the middle, apparently $O(h^3)$, cross term is
in fact $O(h^4)$: this holds because $\Cov_{\tilde q_h}(u,Q(u))$ itself vanishes at
$h=0$ and is only $O(h)$, not $O(1)$. Indeed, since $Q(u)$ is a quadratic form in
$u$, $\E_{\tilde q_h}[u\,Q(u)^\top]$ and $\E_{\tilde q_h}[u]\,\E_{\tilde q_h}[Q(u)]^\top$
both involve only the third moments $\E_{\tilde q_h}[u_au_bu_c]$ of $\tilde q_h$ (for
the second factor, through $\E_{\tilde q_h}[u]=O(h)$ paired with
$\E_{\tilde q_h}[Q(u)]=O(1)$, which is itself an $O(h)$ quantity overall); expanding
\eqref{eq:tilted-u} to the relevant order,
\[
\E_{\tilde q_h}[u_au_bu_c]=\E_\varphi[u_au_bu_c]+h\sum_e s_e(y)\,\E_\varphi[u_au_bu_cu_e]+O(h^2)
=0+h\big[s_c(y)\delta_{ab}+s_b(y)\delta_{ac}+s_a(y)\delta_{bc}\big]+O(h^2),
\]
using $\E_\varphi[u_au_bu_c]=0$ and the fourth-moment identity above. Thus every
third moment of $\tilde q_h$ is itself $O(h)$ (it is exactly $0$ under the
untilted Gaussian $\varphi$), so $\Cov_{\tilde q_h}(u,Q(u))=O(h)$, and the cross
term in \eqref{eq:expansion-terms} is $h^3\cdot O(h)=O(h^4)$, absorbed into the
stated $O(h^4)$. The final term $h^4\Cov_{\tilde q_h}[Q(u)]=O(h^4)$ trivially.
Combining, $\Cov_h[\mu(Y)]=h^2J(y)J(y)^\top+O(h^4)$, the between-group term of
\eqref{eq:expansion}.

Substituting Steps~2--3 into \eqref{eq:expansion-totcov} proves
\eqref{eq:expansion-pop}, i.e.\ the deterministic part of \eqref{eq:expansion}.

\emph{Step 4: Monte-Carlo fluctuation.} Write $S_N:=\frac1N\sum_jK_h(y-y^j)$ and
$T_N:=\frac1N\sum_jK_h(y-y^j)x^j$, so $\bar x_h(y)=T_N/S_N$. Each is an average of
$N$ i.i.d.\ terms; a standard kernel-density computation gives
$\Var\big(K_h(y-Y)\big)=\Theta(h^{-k})$ (the kernel has height $\Theta(h^{-k})$ on an
effective support of Lebesgue measure $\Theta(h^k)$, so its second moment is
$\Theta(h^{-k})\cdot\Theta(h^k)\cdot\Theta(h^{-k})=\Theta(h^{-k})$, dominating the
squared mean $\Theta(1)$), and likewise for $K_h(y-Y)X$. Hence
$S_N=\E[K_h(y-Y)]+O_P\big(\sqrt{h^{-k}/N}\big)$ and
$T_N=\E[K_h(y-Y)X]+O_P\big(\sqrt{h^{-k}/N}\big)$, i.e.\ each has \emph{relative}
fluctuation $O_P\big((Nh^k)^{-1/2}\big)$ around a mean of order $\Theta(1)$. A
first-order Taylor (Slutsky) expansion of the ratio $T_N/S_N$ around the population
ratio $\E_h[X]$ then gives $\bar x_h(y)=\E_h[X]+O_P\big((Nh^k)^{-1/2}\big)$, exactly
as in standard Nadaraya--Watson consistency theory; the same argument applied to the
(also ratio-of-averages) quadratic functional $\Covh(y)$ gives
$\Covh(y)=\Cov_h^{\mathrm{pop}}(y)+O_P\big((Nh^k)^{-1/2}\big)$, which is the
remaining term of \eqref{eq:expansion} and completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:liplb}]
Let $X,X'$ be i.i.d.\ copies of $X_t\mid I=i\sim\N(tx^i,(1-t)^2I_d)$ and
$e(\cdot)=f_i(\cdot,t)-v(\cdot,t,y^i)$. Since
$f_i(X,t)-f_i(X',t)=-(X-X')/(1-t)$ and $X-X'\sim\N(0,2(1-t)^2I_d)$,
\[
\E|f_i(X,t)-f_i(X',t)|^2=\frac{2(1-t)^2d}{(1-t)^2}=2d.
\]
By $L$-Lipschitzness, $\E|v(X,t,y^i)-v(X',t,y^i)|^2\le L^2\E|X-X'|^2=2dL^2(1-t)^2$.
Also $\E|e(X)-e(X')|^2=2\E|e(X)|^2-2|\E e(X)|^2\le2\E|e(X)|^2$ (since $X,X'$ are
i.i.d.). Applying the $L^2(\Omega)$ triangle inequality to
$f_i(X,t)-f_i(X',t)=[e(X)-e(X')]+[v(X,t,y^i)-v(X',t,y^i)]$,
\[
\sqrt{2d}\ \le\ \sqrt{2\,\E|e(X)|^2}\ +\ \sqrt{2d}\,L(1-t),
\]
hence $\sqrt{\E|e(X)|^2}\ge\sqrt d\,[1-L(1-t)]_+$, i.e.\ $\E|e(X)|^2\ge d[1-L(1-t)]_+^2$,
which is \eqref{eq:floor}'s pointwise bound with $e(X)=v(X_t,t,y^i)-f_i(X_t,t)$ up to
sign. (Using the sharp identity $\E|e(X)-e(X')|^2=2\E|e(X)|^2-2|\E e(X)|^2\le2\E|e(X)|^2$,
rather than the cruder bound $4\E|e(X)|^2$, is what preserves the constant $d$ in
\eqref{eq:floor}; a weaker triangle-inequality step would give only a smaller floor.)
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:floor}]
Substituting $s=1-t$, $\int_0^{1/L}(1-Ls)^2\,ds=\big[s-Ls^2+\tfrac{L^2s^3}3\big]_0^{1/L}
=\tfrac1L-\tfrac1L+\tfrac1{3L}=\tfrac1{3L}$. Proposition~\ref{prop:liplb}'s bound
\eqref{eq:floor}'s integrand holds for every training index $i$, hence for every
$v\in\mathcal F_L$ and every $t\in[1-1/L,1)$, so it survives averaging over
$I\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\dots,N\}$ and $t\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)$, giving
$\inf_{v\in\mathcal F_L}L_0(v)\ge d\int_{1-1/L}^1[1-L(1-t)]^2\,dt=d/(3L)$.
\end{proof}
