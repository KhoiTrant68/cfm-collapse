At any finite time, the spatial derivative of a softmax mean is `(t/(1-t)^2) Cov(a)`. The finite atom set uniformly bounds that covariance independently of the label coefficients. On every compact time interval below one, this provides an integrable dominating constant for differentiation under an arbitrary probability measure. Pointwise continuity of the derivatives and dominated convergence give a continuous spatial derivative of the averaged field. The same argument includes time zero, where the derivative of the mean vanishes. Thus the stated spatial C1 regularity and local Lipschitz property do hold even for arbitrarily heavy label tails. The bounded mean gives linear growth for both time directions on each finite interval, so the finite-time maps are global C1 diffeomorphisms. Uniform control as time tends to one is neither asserted nor needed.

2. **Concentration and compact uniformity.** For a sequence tending to a point z, every atom outside N(z) has a fixed positive squared-distance gap eventually. For each fixed label, its positive coefficient ratios are finite, so the exponential gap defeats them. The weights are bounded by one; dominated convergence therefore survives arbitrary averaging. This also supplies the compact-uniform statement used to bound speed from below: if that statement failed on a compact set disjoint from C, take a convergent subsequence of the offending points, apply the just-established sequential concentration, and obtain a limiting mean in conv N(z). Vanishing speed then forces z into C, a contradiction. Uniform bounds on coefficient ratios across labels are unnecessary. An attempted objection based on unbounded label tails therefore does not refute the argument.

3. **Finitely many equidistant candidates.** The relevant assertion includes the restriction to aff S. If z and z' are both in aff S and equidistant from every point of S, subtraction of the equal-distance equations shows that z-z' is orthogonal to span(S-S); membership in aff S makes the same difference belong to that span. Hence z=z'. Affine dependence causes possible nonexistence, not nonuniqueness. Without the aff S restriction, this claim would be false, but both propositions retain that restriction. Taking finitely many nonempty subsets proves finiteness of C.

If the trajectory never leaves, the lower speed bound itself contradicts finite energy. The bounded continuous trajectory has a nonempty compact connected omega-limit set, because closures of connected trajectory tails are nested compact connected sets. A subset of the finite set C with these properties is a singleton. Finite energy alone would not establish convergence, but the proof supplies the needed additional steps.
